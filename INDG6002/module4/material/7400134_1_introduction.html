Introduction

Original Blackboard item · _7400134_1

Cultural encounters with clients in healthcare settings can be challenging for healthcare professionals who are often effected by fear and apprehension.

For Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples the consequence of past experiences of institutionalised, internalised, and interpersonal racism within healthcare settings and other government or formal organisations have led to avoidance of mainstream services.

There remains a clear lack of knowledge and understanding of antecedents and the ongoing impacts that are the bases for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples fear and avoidance of accessing services. Originating from colonisation and exacerbated by institutional racism (see Module 2), Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander clients continue to be reluctant to engage with all Australian government organisations (Durey, Thompson & Wood, 2012; Rix, Moran, Kapeen & Wilson, 2016). 

This module will provide you with a cultural safety framework for working in equitable, culturally safe, and respectful ways in service settings when working with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples. Today, disconnect between the cultural values and worldviews of Indigenous Peoples and non-indigenous Australians is still apparent. Disconnection extends beyond personal relationships into 'mainstream' organisations providing services that reflect institutionalised culture.
