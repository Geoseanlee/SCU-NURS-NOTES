‘Hidden’ history

The British took Australia under the lie of ‘terra nullius’ (land belonging to no one) and immediately commenced the systematic removal of Traditional Owners from Country. Dispossession, massacres and warfare, along with diseases introduced by colonisers, took an immense toll on First Nations populations within only several decades of colonisation (Best & Fredericks, 2019).

 ‘Terra nullius’ remained as an active government policy until the Mabo Decision in the High Court in 1992. The Mabo Case was a landmark legal case in Australia that recognised the land rights of the Meriam people, traditional owners of the Murray Islands (which include the islands of Mer, Dauer and Waier) in the Torres Strait. The Mabo Case was successful in overturning the myth that at the time of colonisation Australia was ‘terra nullius’ or 'land belonging to no one'. The High Court finally recognised that Indigenous peoples had lived in Australia for thousands of years and enjoyed rights to their land according to their own laws and customs.

Activity: Reflection

If the land taken by the British was not deemed as ‘belonging to anyone’ until 1992, from a colonisers perspective, where did that position Traditional Owners if they were not deemed to be ‘anyone’ ?

From the point of first contact with the British there was no organised effort to recognise the Aboriginal population, which was severly impacted by new diseases and frontier violence. However, previous estimates of Australia’s pre-colonial population ranged from 300,000 to 1 million. By 1900 the Aboriginal population is estimated to have been reduced by 84%, although it remains difficult to find accurate statistics on Aboriginal populations (Butlin, 1982). Still today it remains challenging to accurately record how many Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples live within Australia. Some First Nations Peoples choose not to identify, due to a lack of trust in all government services and processes. Fear and avoidance stems from Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people’s experiences of discrimination, racism, and ongoing removal of children by state authorities.
