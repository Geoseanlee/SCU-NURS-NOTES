## The central contrast

The western biomedical lens tends to organise care around the individual body, disease and treatment. The Indigenous health definitions presented in the module connect physical, social, emotional, cultural and spiritual wellbeing with family, community and Country. These are not interchangeable definitions of an isolated patient. Avoid treating one account as a universal description of every Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander person.

## Social and Emotional Wellbeing (SEWB)

Read SEWB as a relational and holistic framework rather than a synonym for psychiatric symptoms. Country, family, culture and spiritual connections matter alongside bodily health. The module links its development with Indigenous scholarship, community-controlled health and the wider primary-health-care movement.

## Listening and communication

Dadirri emphasises deep inner listening and quiet awareness. Yarning uses attentive listening and space for a person’s story rather than a rapid checklist of questions. Clinical yarning connects relationship-building with clinical understanding; it does not remove the duty to explain, obtain consent and check understanding.

## Self-determination

Article 3 of UNDRIP provides a connection between collective rights and health: people determine their political status and pursue their economic, social and cultural development. Use the original reading and module discussion to explain the connection rather than reducing self-determination to accepting a pre-set treatment choice.

## Revision prompts

Compare a biomedical account and a SEWB account of the same health encounter. Explain how Country can be a determinant of wellbeing. Identify how your own worldview shapes the questions you ask. Read Terare and Rawsthorne and Lin, Green and Bessarab alongside the module’s examples.

## Keep these distinctions clear

| Concept | What to explain | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Holistic health | Relationships between physical, emotional, social, cultural and spiritual wellbeing, family, community and Country | Treating Indigenous health as a checklist appended to a biomedical assessment |
| SEWB | A relational framework with cultural and collective dimensions | Using SEWB only as a substitute term for mental illness |
| Dadirri and yarning | Listening, relationship and a person’s story shape understanding | Assuming one communication style applies to every Indigenous person |
| Self-determination | Who defines priorities and holds decision-making authority | Reducing choice to accepting a plan already fixed by a service |
