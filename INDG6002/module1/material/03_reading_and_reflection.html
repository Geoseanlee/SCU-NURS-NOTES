Reading 1.1

Please complete the reading below via MyReadings:

Terare, M., & Rawsthorne, M. (2019). Country Is Yarning to Me: Worldview, Health and Well-Being Amongst Australian First Nations People. The British Journal Of Social Work, 50(3). doi:10.1093/bjsw/bcz072

This paper is written by Mareese Terare, a social worker/academic and Aboriginal woman from the Bundjalung and Geonpul Nations, with her co-author Margot Rawsthorne, a descendant of a convict who arrived in the colony in the1820s and settled on Wiradjuri Country in Central Western NSW. The authors describe for you the power of Indigenous ways of knowing and knowledges in addressing health inequities. They present Country as the under-acknowledged social determinant of health and well-being. For Mareese and her People, Country is so much more than a physical place. It is a place of belonging, where recognition of trauma, diversity and the power of Yarning and storytelling can be healing and create pathways to understanding and the building of positive relationships and mutually enhanced wellbeing. Yarning and deep listening are crucial here. Australian Elder of the Year (2021) Dr Miriam Rose Ungunmerr says Dadirri (deep listening) “may enable health and social workers to listen, learn and act in ways that promote the well-being of Australia’s First Nations People” (Terare & Rawsthorne, 2019 p. 957). Dr Miriam-Rose Ungunmerr has gifted her Dadirri practice to all Australians, regardless of racial or cultural backgrounds.
