## Social determinants

Health is shaped by the conditions in which people are born, grow, work, live and age, and by the systems producing those conditions. The module covers income, education, employment, housing, food security, early childhood, inclusion and access to appropriate services.

## An Indigenous lens

The course explicitly adds history, racism, Country, human rights and policy processes to a narrow socioeconomic checklist. Describe structural pathways rather than substituting cultural stereotypes for an explanation.

## Cultural determinants are strengths

Connections to Country, language, culture, identity, family and community can protect wellbeing. Cultural determinants refer to the meanings defined by people and communities, not simply the organisational culture of a hospital. A strengths-based account does not deny the material effects of poverty or discrimination.

## The social gradient and epigenetics

The module uses a social-gradient model to connect cumulative disadvantage and health opportunities. It introduces epigenetics as a topic for reflection: avoid claiming that a brief example proves a deterministic biological explanation for every intergenerational effect.

## Revision prompts

Explain how income, housing and discrimination can interact in one person’s access to care. Reframe a deficit statement as a strengths-based question without hiding structural injustice. Identify whose definition of culture is being used and who controls decisions.

## Social and cultural determinants together

| Lens | Examples in the module | Analytical task |
|---|---|---|
| Material/social | Housing, income, education, employment, food security and early life | Explain pathways linking conditions to exposure, access and opportunity |
| Political/historical | Racism, policy, human rights and the effects of colonisation | Ask how those conditions are produced and maintained |
| Cultural strengths | Country, language, identity, kinship and community connection | Describe strengths on communities’ terms rather than reducing culture to risk |
| Interaction | Material disadvantage and cultural strength may coexist | Avoid both deficit-only narratives and the claim that cultural strength removes the need for structural change |
