## More than political independence

In this module decolonisation also concerns recognising, legitimising and rebuilding Indigenous knowledges, cultures, values and governance. It requires acknowledgement that settler-colonial assumptions persist in contemporary health and social services.

## Begin with your own lens

Examine how education, professional status and dominant cultural norms shape your authority. Cultural safety provides a way to question practices that are presented as universal or neutral. Critical reflexivity is ongoing, not a one-off statement of good intentions.

## Relational and structural change

Trust requires sustained relationships and action on power and decision-making. Positive wording in a policy is not evidence that day-to-day practice changed. Centre Indigenous ways of knowing, being and doing and meaningful community leadership.

## Media and deficit narratives

The module examines how problem-focused representation can stereotype communities, damage identity and reinforce fear of services. Strengths-based representation should recognise capacity and self-determination without concealing racism or unmet needs.

## Revision prompts

Use the Green and Bennett Wayanha reading to identify one assumption to challenge, one practice to change and one structural barrier requiring collective action. Ask how the community—not only the practitioner—would recognise progress.

## Three levels of decolonising practice

| Level | What must be examined | Weak substitute to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Personal | Assumptions about legitimate knowledge, expertise and normal practice | A one-off declaration of good intentions |
| Relational | Trust, listening, whose voice is centred and who can refuse | Consultation after all important choices have been made |
| Structural | Governance, resource control, policy and accountability | Changing terminology while leaving power unchanged |

For the media activity, identify who speaks, whose expertise is omitted and whether the story frames people only as a problem. A strengths-based alternative should preserve truth about injustice while recognising agency and community-defined priorities.
