Welcome to the unit
-

Unit overview
Raises understanding of Indigenous health issues including: Indigenous concepts and frameworks for health; the negative impact of colonisation on health and wellbeing; cultural safety; and reflection on how to decolonise professional practice. Awareness of best-practice Indigenous health care strategies will be elevated through discussion, critical analysis, and service planning exercises.
Unit Learning Outcomes (ULOs)
By the end of this unit, you will be able to:
- ULO 1 Synthesise key concepts of health and wellbeing as determined by Aboriginal People
- ULO 2 critically analyse key stages of the colonisation process and its relationship to the contemporary health status of Australian Indigenous people
- ULO 3 critically analyse the relationship between socio-economic factors and the contemporary health status of Australian Indigenous people
- ULO 4 reflect on and synthesise how primary health care and/or health promotion enhances the wellbeing of Indigenous people and role of the health professional as an agent of social change

Dr Darlene Rotumah
PhD | MIndgSt | BA (Welfare Studies)
Darlene is a Bundjalung woman with extensive experience in the health sector. She is dedicated to strengthening students’ understanding of how to build respectful, culturally informed, and trusting relationships with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples across health and social service contexts. Drawing on Indigenous knowledge systems, cultural practices, and community‑centred approaches to learning, she supports students to recognise the depth, cultural realities, and enduring cultural presence of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander worldviews. Her teaching encourages future practitioners to engage with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples in ways that honour cultural authority, promote self‑determination, and uphold culturally safe care.

Academic Integrity at Southern Cross University
Academic integrity is a core expectation for every student at SCU. Whenever you submit work, you are required to follow the Student Academic and Non-Academic Misconduct Rules. These Rules explain the standards for honest scholarship and the consequences if standards are breached. You are responsible for understanding these expectations and applying them in all assessments across your studies.
Not following the Rules can lead to serious penalties, including failing an assessment, failing the unit, or more significant University sanctions. If you ever feel unsure about what academic integrity means in your unit, speak with your Unit Assessor or visit the Academic Integrity website for more guidance.
Start the Academic Integrity Module
If you are starting your SCU studies, you must complete the Academic Integrity Module during your first study period. This module helps you understand academic integrity, the responsible use of sources, referencing, collaboration, and how to avoid common mistakes in academic integrity.
If you do not complete the module:
- Your grades may be withheld
- You may be withdrawn from future enrolled units
- You may be unable to enrol in further study
These penalties remain in effect until the module is successfully completed.
Completing the module usually takes 1 to 2 hours, so it’s best to complete it early in the session.
