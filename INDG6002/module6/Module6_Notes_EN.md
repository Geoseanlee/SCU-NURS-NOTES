## Primary health care (PHC)

PHC takes a social and whole-of-life view of health. It links meeting health needs with action on wider determinants and enabling people, families and communities to influence their health. Accessibility, affordability, acceptability and community direction matter alongside clinical effectiveness.

## Health promotion

Health promotion supports people to increase control over their health. Revisit the Ottawa Charter’s actions: healthy public policy, supportive environments, community action, personal skills and reorienting services. It is broader than giving individuals lifestyle instructions.

## Community control

The module places Aboriginal Community Controlled Health Organisations at the centre of culturally relevant PHC. Redfern’s Aboriginal Medical Service opened in 1971, preceding the 1978 Alma-Ata declaration. Community control is about governance and priorities, not merely locating a mainstream service in a community.

## Aboriginal Health Workers

These workers contribute clinical, cultural, communication, outreach and liaison expertise. Work alongside them respectfully; do not reduce their contribution to translation or use them to transfer the whole organisation’s responsibility for cultural safety.

## Bring modules together

Connect SEWB, colonisation, social and cultural determinants, cultural safety and decolonising practice to service design. Ask who sets priorities, who controls resources, how outcomes are defined and whether access and relationships improve from the community’s perspective.

## PHC, promotion and community control

| Concept | Key distinction | Practice question |
|---|---|---|
| Primary health care | Broader than a first-contact clinical appointment; includes determinants and participation | Is care accessible, affordable, acceptable and connected to community priorities? |
| Health promotion | More than individual advice | Which Ottawa Charter action changes policy, environments, community power, skills or services? |
| Community control | Governance and authority, not merely proximity or consultation | Who owns the priorities, allocates resources and defines success? |
| Aboriginal Health Workers | Clinical, cultural, relationship and liaison expertise | Is the whole organisation sharing responsibility for cultural safety? |

Use all six modules together: define health through the relevant community lens, explain historical and structural conditions, recognise strengths, assess power and safety, and examine community control. Avoid calling a service culturally safe solely because it employs an Indigenous worker.
