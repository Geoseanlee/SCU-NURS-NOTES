## ppt/slides/slide9.xml
Primary Health Care (PHC)
Primary Health Care is a whole-of-society approach to health that aims at ensuring the highest possible level of health and wellbeing and their equitable distribution by focusing on people’s needs as early as possible along the continuum from health promotion and disease prevention to treatment, rehabilitation, and palliative care, and as close as feasible to people’s everyday environment.
WHO, 2023 

## ppt/slides/slide10.xml
Primary Health Care (PHC) continued …
A primary health care approach includes three components:
Meeting people’s health needs throughout their lives,
Addressing the broader determinants of health through multisectoral policy and action, and
Empowering individuals, families, and communities to take charge of their own health.

## ppt/slides/slide12.xml
Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples Definition of:
Primary Health Care (PHC)
“Primary health care (PHC)
 is … generally the first ‘layer’ of health care services that individuals, families and the community encounter that usually provide comprehensive and continuous care. Primary health care includes general practitioners (GPs), Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Health Workers and Practitioners, nurses, allied health professionals, midwives, pharmacists and dentists. The services provided as part of primary health care are broad, and include health promotion and education, prevention and screening, early intervention, treatment and management.”
https://healthinfonet.ecu.edu.au/learn/health-system/primary-health-care/

## ppt/slides/slide13.xml
NACCHO
Peak national body representing 146 ACCHO’s in urban, rural and remote areas
“A process which allows the local Aboriginal community to be involved in its affairs in accordance with whatever protocols or procedures are determined by the Community”
Objectives
To ameliorate poverty within the Aboriginal community through self determination
Promote, develop and expand health services through ACCHS
Represent and advocate for health service delivery, health information, research, public health, health financing and programs
To deliver holistic and culturally appropriate health and health-related services to the Aboriginal community.
https://www.naccho.org.au/acchos/

## ppt/slides/slide14.xml
Recap: ACCHO
An ACCHO is a primary health care service initiated and operated by the local Aboriginal community to deliver holistic, comprehensive, and culturally appropriate health care 
to the community which controls it
, through a locally elected Board of Management.

## ppt/slides/slide15.xml
Coomealla Health Aboriginal Corporation’s Community‑Controlled Primary Care Model (Wentworth, NSW)
What Happened:
Wentworth lost both GP clinics in 2018 → major access crisis.
CHAC (an ACCHO) stepped in to establish a new primary care clinic.
Aboriginal board led all decisions: service design, staffing, priorities.
Clinic built around holistic, culturally grounded care.
Salaried GP model + Medicare 19(2) exemption ensured sustainability.
Outreach and allied health integrated across multiple towns.
Why It Matters:
Self‑determination:
 Indigenous governance drives system redesign.
Holistic health:
 Care reflects SEWB, family, culture, and community.
Cultural safety:
 Longer appointments, relational practice, trusted environment.
Strengths‑based health promotion:
 Delivered through relationships, not pamphlets.
Community‑controlled solutions:
 Structural innovation replaces failed mainstream models.
Improved outcomes:
 Access, continuity, trust, and community engagement strengthened.
https://www.chac.org.au/post/welcome-to-coomealla-health-aboriginal-corporation-chac-community-controlled-healthcare-in-dareto

## ppt/slides/slide29.xml
A human rights-based approach to health
Human rights provide a framework for addressing the consequences of health inequality experienced by First Nations Peoples. 
This includes recognising its underlying causes as well as the interconnections with other issues. 
Human rights require more than a rhetorical acknowledgement of the existence of inequality and general commitments to overcome this situation at some unspecified time in the future.
Key elements include: 
Availability, Accessibility, Non-discrimination
The 
National Strategic Framework
 identifies as a key result area achieving a health system that is more effective and responsive to First Nations Peoples needs.

## ppt/slides/slide42.xml
INDG6002 GenAI Use
In Gnibi College units, the use of GenAI in assessments is generally restricted to protect the integrity and authenticity of the learning process. Indigenous Knowledges are grounded in lived experience, cultural practices, and relational ways of knowing. Engaging meaningfully with these knowledges requires personal reflection, respectful interpretation, and a commitment to culturally responsive learning—something that cannot be authentically achieved through AI-generated content.
