10 Key Steps for Strong Short Response Writing
Start with a clear main point: 
Emphasise that short responses must begin with the answer, not background. Example sentence starters such as: 
“This concept refers to…”
 or 
“In the scenario, this is evident when…”
.
2. Explain the key concept briefly:  D
efine or clarify the concept in their own words before applying it. This prevents vague or incorrect use of terms 
3. Link the concept to UNDRIP Article 3
 
Show clearly how the concept relates to the rights described in Article 3 and to Australian 
Indigenous Peoples 
understandings of health and wellbeing. Interpret the connection—do not retell general background information.
4. Use scholarly or unit evidence: 
Short responses still require academic grounding. Evidence can be a unit concept, framework, or scholarly idea. Keep it brief. 
5. Write concisely and coherently: 
Use short, direct sentences - improves clarity and reduces grammatical errors - avoid long introductions, unnecessary detail, or narrative writing.

Highlight typical issues: over‑description, missing the main point, unclear links to Article 3, lack of evidence, incorrect terminology, and overly long sentences.

Encourage verbs that show analysis and interpretation:

Assessment 1 - Instructions
 
Step 1: Read UNDRIP Article 3
Consider what the term 
self-determination
 means and why it is important for Australian Indigenous Peoples.
Step 2: Review Unit Content
Use learning materials from:
Module 1: Australian Indigenous concepts of health and wellbeing.
Module 2: Colonisation and its impacts on Australian Indigenous health and wellbeing.
Step 3: Research
Locate additional scholarly literature that supports your discussion.
Definition of Scholarly and Peer-Reviewed sources
Peer-reviewed sources are academic publications that have been evaluated by experts before publication to ensure quality, accuracy, and credibility. Scholarly sources are written by researchers or experts for an academic audience and include journal articles, academic books, conference papers, and theses. They typically provide author credentials and references. All peer-reviewed sources are scholarly, but not all scholarly sources are peer-reviewed. For example, an academic book may be scholarly without undergoing peer review.
Step 4: Write Your Response
Your response should explain how Australian Indigenous concepts of health and wellbeing, self-determination, and community-accepted communication styles are interconnected, and how these relate to the ongoing impacts of colonisation on Australian Indigenous health and wellbeing.

GNIBI GenAI use
 
In Gnibi College units, the use of GenAI in assessments is generally 
restricted
 to protect the 
integrity and authenticity of the learning process.
 Indigenous Knowledges are grounded in lived experience, cultural practices, and relational ways of knowing. Engaging meaningfully with these knowledges requires personal reflection, respectful interpretation, and a commitment to culturally responsive learning, something that cannot be authentically achieved through AI-generated content.
 Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) tools, such as ChatGPT, 
may not be used 
for this Assessment Task. 
You must demonstrate that you have developed the required knowledge and skills 
independently
, without the assistance of GenAI tools. 
Unauthorised
 use of GenAI
 may be considered a breach of academic integrity under the Student Academic and Non-Academic Misconduct Rules (Section 3).’
