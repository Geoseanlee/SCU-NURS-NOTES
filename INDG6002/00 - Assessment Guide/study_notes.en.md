# INDG6002 — Assessment Guide

## Overview

INDG6002 is graded through two individual tasks. Assessment 1 is a Short Response worth 40%, due Week 3, with a 1,000-word limit. Assessment 2 is a Critical Review worth 60%, due Week 6, with a 2,200-word limit. Both are submitted individually as Word documents with a completed coversheet at the start. [S5][S7]

The tasks measure the same core ideas at increasing depth. The unit-level theme is the relationship between self-determination, as articulated in Article 3 of UNDRIP, and Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander understandings of health and wellbeing. Assessment 1 examines Indigenous concepts of health and wellbeing, the contribution of a cultural determinant, community-accepted communication styles, and the enduring impacts of colonisation on Indigenous health outcomes. Assessment 2 keeps these threads and adds socioeconomic determinants of health, primary health care, and health promotion, applied to a clinical scenario. [S1][S5][S7]

As a Gnibi College unit, both briefs also place tight conditions on generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) use. [S5][S7]

## What you must be able to do

The briefs map each task to Unit Learning Outcomes (ULOs), so a submission can only earn marks by demonstrating these abilities:

- Discuss key concepts of health and wellbeing as determined by Aboriginal people — required in both tasks. [S5][S7]
- Identify key stages of the colonisation process and its relationship to the contemporary health status of Australian Indigenous people — required in both tasks. [S5][S7]
- Analyse the relationship between socio-economic factors and the contemporary health status of Australian Indigenous people — required in Assessment 2. [S7]
- Explain how primary health care and/or health promotion enhances the wellbeing of Indigenous people, and the role of the health professional as an agent of social change — required in Assessment 2. [S7]

Alongside the ULOs, both briefs demand working skills in each submission: locating and citing scholarly and peer-reviewed literature, writing and referencing in APA 7th style, applying respectful terminology when referring to Australian Indigenous people, and using GenAI only within the scope the Unit Assessor permits. [S5][S7]

## 1. Assessment 1 — Short Response (40%)

### 1.1 Task details

The brief sets Assessment 1 as: unit INDG6002; type Short Response; due Friday 13th November, Week 3, 11:59 pm AEST/AEDT (10:59 pm Queensland); weight 40%; length 1,000 words; individual. [S5]

### 1.2 What the task asks

The task invites critical analysis of the relationship between self-determination, as articulated in Article 3 of UNDRIP, and Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander understandings of health and wellbeing. Using unit content and scholarly evidence, you examine Indigenous concepts of health and wellbeing, evaluate the contribution of a cultural determinant, analyse the significance of community-accepted communication styles, and reflect critically on the enduring impacts of colonisation on Indigenous health outcomes. [S1][S5]

"Critically analyse" carries real weight: the task is an argument about how self-determination and Indigenous health concepts connect, not a description of them. [S5]

### 1.3 Why the task is shaped this way

The brief's rationale treats understanding Australian Indigenous perspectives of health and wellbeing as essential for culturally safe health practice. The task introduces self-determination, a key principle in the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (UNDRIP), and asks how Indigenous understandings of health differ from Western biomedical approaches and how colonisation has affected Australian Indigenous Peoples' ability to exercise self-determination. It builds skills needed by health, social service, and community-based professionals. [S5]

### 1.4 The four steps in the brief

1. **Read UNDRIP Article 3.** Consider what self-determination means and why it matters for Australian Indigenous Peoples. Article 3 anchors the whole response. [S5]
2. **Review unit content** from Module 1 (Australian Indigenous concepts of health and wellbeing) and Module 2 (colonisation and its impacts on Australian Indigenous health and wellbeing). These two modules supply the concepts your discussion must connect. [S5]
3. **Research.** Locate additional scholarly literature. The brief defines the terms: peer-reviewed sources have been evaluated by experts before publication to ensure quality, accuracy, and credibility, while scholarly sources are written by researchers or experts for an academic audience and include journal articles, academic books, conference papers, and theses. All peer-reviewed sources are scholarly, but not all scholarly sources are peer-reviewed — an academic book may be scholarly without undergoing peer review. [S5]
4. **Write the response.** The brief specifies the target: an explanation of how Australian Indigenous concepts of health and wellbeing, self-determination, and community-accepted communication styles are interconnected, and how these relate to the ongoing impacts of colonisation on Australian Indigenous health and wellbeing. [S5]

Step 4's formulation means the response must weave all strands into one explanation, not answer them as four mini-essays. [S5]

### 1.5 Referencing, format and resources

Assessment 1 uses APA 7th citations and referencing, guided by the SCU Library referencing guides and the "APA 7th Document Formatting" Quick Guide. The brief and resource page name these materials: INDG6002 A1 Brief.pdf and INDG6002 A1 Rubric.pdf; UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples UNDRIP 2007.pdf (needed for Step 1); Quick Guide Writing a Short Response 2025.pdf; Key steps for strong short response writing.pdf; Example Structure Assessment 1.pdf; Accepted Terminology Guide Tone Apostrophes INDG6002.pdf; Types of Academic Sources – Peer-Reviewed, Scholarly, Authoritative.pdf; coversheet INDG6002.docx; Tracking Grammarly template.docx; plus other resources: SCU Quick Guides, Common instruction words in assessments, APA 7th Document Formatting Guides, APA 7th Reference Guide, and Special Consideration / Extensions. [S5][S6]

### 1.6 Marking

The brief states the rubric outlines the grading criteria and the standards expected at each grade level, and directs you to download INDG6002 A1 Rubric.pdf; it also refers to "Final Grades" for further information. The criteria themselves sit in the rubric PDF, not in the assessment documents in this corpus — see Source gaps. [S5][S6]

## 2. Assessment 2 — Critical Review (60%)

### 2.1 Task details

The brief sets Assessment 2 as: unit INDG6002; type Critical Review; due Week 6, Friday 4th December, 11:59 pm (NSW, AEDT / AEST); weight 60%; length 2,200 words; individual. [S7]

### 2.2 What the task asks

Assessment 2 is a 2,200-word critical review built on the scenario of Sarah, an Aboriginal woman accessing healthcare services. Drawing on the unit's concepts, you critically analyse Australian Indigenous understandings of health and wellbeing, the impacts of colonisation, the influence of socioeconomic determinants of health, and the role of primary health care and health promotion in supporting positive health outcomes. [S7]

You must also evaluate how culturally safe, strengths-based healthcare practices can enhance Australian Indigenous health and wellbeing, and provide evidence-based recommendations for culturally safe care, self-determination, and improved health outcomes, in a clear, coherent, respectful academic style, supported by a minimum of 12 scholarly sources. [S7]

Two features separate the Critical Review from the Short Response: it anchors your analysis in evidence from the scenario, the scholarly literature, and unit materials, and its recommendations ask for positions a practitioner could act on. [S7]

### 2.3 Why the task is shaped this way

The brief's rationale frames the review as an application task: unit concepts and theories are applied to a realistic healthcare scenario to examine how culturally safe, strengths-based, and socially responsive practices can support Australian Indigenous health and wellbeing. The task pushes beyond deficit-focused approaches by recognising strengths, resilience, cultural connections, and self-determination as foundations of Indigenous health and wellbeing, and asks you to weigh up health professionals' role as agents of social change for culturally safe and equitable care. [S7]

### 2.4 Suggested structure and word budget

The brief supplies a structure whose approximate word allocations total the 2,200-word limit, so treat them as your drafting budget:

| Section | Approx. words | ULO focus | Required content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction | 220 | — | Introduce the scenario; identify the key issues; outline the main focus |
| Australian Indigenous Health and Wellbeing | 630 | ULO 1 | Apply Australian Indigenous concepts of health and wellbeing to the experiences, strengths, and circumstances in the scenario |
| Primary Health Care, Health Promotion and Recommendations | 630 | ULO 4 | Critically examine how primary health care and health promotion can enhance health and wellbeing; give evidence-based recommendations for culturally safe practice from the scenario |
| Colonisation, Socioeconomic Determinants of Health and Contemporary Health | 500 | ULO 2 & 3 | Examine how colonisation has influenced the social, economic, and environmental factors affecting Indigenous health; apply the discussion to Sarah's experiences; explain how your learning can inform actions in your professional practice to improve health outcomes |
| Conclusion | 220 | — | Summarise key arguments and recommendations; no new information |

[S7]

Each body section maps to a different ULO, so a section that runs long starves its ULO's marks. The conclusion restriction is explicit. [S7]

### 2.5 Writing and evidence requirements

The brief lists conditions the review must satisfy: a clear, coherent, and respectful academic style; evidence from scholarly literature and unit materials; evidence from a minimum of 12 scholarly sources; appropriate terminology and respectful language for Australian Indigenous people; evidence-backed argument and critical analysis throughout; and academic writing conventions including accurate referencing and citation. The 12-source minimum is a floor: spread across five sections that must cover concepts, colonisation, socioeconomic determinants, primary health care, and health promotion, it is the least viable coverage. [S7]

### 2.6 Referencing and resources

Assessment 2 uses APA 7th style for citations and referencing, with the SCU Library referencing guides and the "APA 7th Document Formatting" Quick Guide. The resource page names INDG6002 A2 Brief.pdf and INDG6002 A2 Rubric.pdf and links: a Reflective Writing Guide, SCU Quick Guides, Common instruction words in assessments, APA 7th Document Formatting Guides, APA 7th Reference Guide, and Special Consideration / Extensions. [S7][S8]

### 2.7 Marking

As with Assessment 1, criterion-level detail sits in INDG6002 A2 Rubric .pdf rather than in the assessment documents in this corpus — see Source gaps. [S7][S8]

## 3. Assurance and declarations

### 3.1 Assessment assurance

The unit warns that submissions may be used for quality assurance and peer review purposes. Where external reviewers are involved, consent will be sought, and personal information and clinical content are handled under the University's privacy obligations. Your submission may therefore be seen beyond your marker. [S2]

### 3.2 Assessment declaration

By submitting assessments for grading you agree to five conditions: you have read and understood the University's academic integrity rules; the submitted work is your own, properly referenced, and not submitted in another unit; you have followed the University guidelines for GenAI use and the conditions in the assessment brief and/or set by the unit assessor; you understand the consequences of academic misconduct; and the work may be checked for authenticity, with a false declaration being a serious breach of academic integrity. [S3]

### 3.3 GenAI coversheet mechanics

For a Level 2 task, the brief instructs you to choose one option on the coversheet and copy the whole coversheet into the beginning of your Word document; it is not counted in the word count. The declaration includes an acknowledgement that you have read the Assessment Brief, that GenAI conditions vary between assessments, and that failure to demonstrate understanding of the assessment topic or adherence to the Unit Assessor's GenAI requirements can breach academic integrity. [S5][S7]

The coversheet requires assurances. Under "NO": you have not used any GenAI in any part of this assessment, and you still affirm you have kept copies or screenshots of all internet and library catalogue searches and versions of your work, available on request. Under "YES": you used GenAI for the purposes outlined, you acknowledged ALL AI-produced text using the relevant library guides, you stayed within the assessment's limits, you kept copies of all GenAI prompts and outputs plus versions of your work, and you listed how and where you used Grammarly Premium AI. The Tracking Grammarly template named in Assessment 1 resources is the instrument for that list. [S5][S6][S7]

The same coversheet carries an Academic Integrity Declaration: you declare you have read and understood SCU's Academic Integrity policies and referencing guidelines, are aware of the consequences of academic misconduct, confirm the submission is your own original work, referenced appropriately, not previously submitted, authorise its reproduction for authentication purposes, understand the implications of a false declaration, and have adhered to guidelines regarding Generative AI. [S5]

## 4. Academic integrity and GenAI rules

### 4.1 Why Gnibi restricts GenAI

The brief's position is that GenAI use in Gnibi College units is generally restricted to protect the integrity and authenticity of the learning process: Indigenous Knowledges are grounded in lived experience, cultural practices, and relational ways of knowing, and engaging meaningfully with them requires personal reflection, respectful interpretation, and culturally responsive learning that AI-generated content cannot authentically achieve. You must demonstrate the required knowledge and skills independently; unauthorised GenAI use may breach academic integrity under the Student Academic and Non-Academic Misconduct Rules (Section 3). [S5][S7]

### 4.2 Level 2: what GenAI may and may not do

Both assessments are classified as Level 2: AI use permitted for specific purposes identified by the Unit Assessor. Within that classification, Grammarly Premium AI is the only generative software allowed, and only for two purposes: formatting, spelling and grammar — language improvements only, with no content or knowledge creation; and paraphrasing — permitted, with careful attention to adjustments that affect clarity, and paraphrasing using Grammarly Premium AI must be referenced. [S5][S7]

The prohibitions are boundaries of that permission, not soft warnings:

- No use of any other GenAI tool for content or knowledge creation. [S5][S7]
- Do not present GenAI-generated material as your own original ideas. [S5][S7]
- Do not ask GenAI to write any part of the analysis, commentary, or conclusions. [S5][S7]
- Do not upload or provide research article text for GenAI to rewrite, summarise, or interpret. [S5][S7]

The line is drawn at language manipulation: Grammarly can adjust your words, but the analysis behind them must be yours, and anything it paraphrases must be acknowledged by reference. [S5][S7]

## 7. Practical preparation plan

1. **Read the Assessment 1 brief, the coversheet, and UNDRIP Article 3 first.** Step 1 of the instructions is reading Article 3 and considering what self-determination means for Australian Indigenous Peoples; the article anchors everything, so do it before drafting. [S5][S6]
2. **Build notes from Modules 1 and 2**, the two content areas the brief names. Tag each note to the strand it feeds: health and wellbeing concepts, or colonisation and its impacts. [S5]
3. **Research early and sort sources with the scholarly test.** Peer-reviewed means pre-publication expert evaluation; scholarly means written by researchers or experts for an academic audience, with credentials and references; an academic book qualifies as scholarly without peer review. Use the Types of Academic Sources guide. [S5][S6]
4. **Draft Assessment 1 as one connected argument, using the named supports.** Example Structure Assessment 1.pdf and the writing quick guides shape the form; the Accepted Terminology Guide checks tone, apostrophes, and terminology. The response must connect health and wellbeing concepts, self-determination, and communication styles, with colonisation running through them. [S5][S6]
5. **Keep Grammarly Premium AI in its lane and keep evidence as you go.** Permitted: formatting, spelling, grammar, and referenced paraphrasing. Prohibited: content or knowledge creation, any other GenAI tool, AI-written analysis or conclusions, and feeding article text to AI for rewriting or summarising. Build the usage list with the Tracking Grammarly template, and save search screenshots and work versions from the start — the assurance to keep them applies even with the "NO" declaration. [S5][S7]
6. **Assemble and submit Assessment 1 correctly.** Paste the completed coversheet at the start of the Word document, outside the 1,000-word count; format per the APA 7th Document Formatting guide; check references against the APA 7th Reference Guide; upload through Assessment 1 submissions ('View Instructions') before the portal's displayed local deadline. [S5][S6]
7. **Move straight into Assessment 2 planning.** Read the Sarah scenario and note which of its details bear on each area: Indigenous health and wellbeing concepts, colonisation, socioeconomic determinants, and primary health care with health promotion. Build the source list now and count it — you need at least 12 scholarly sources. [S7][S8]
8. **Draft Assessment 2 inside the word budget,** following the brief's order — introduction (about 220), health and wellbeing (about 630), primary health care, health promotion and recommendations (about 630), colonisation and socioeconomic determinants (about 500), conclusion (about 220). Weave Sarah's scenario into every body section, write recommendations a practitioner could act on, and keep the conclusion free of new material. [S7]
9. **Close out terminology, referencing and integrity.** Check against the brief's list: clear academic style, evidence from scholarly literature and unit materials, 12-plus sources, respectful terminology, accurate APA 7th referencing. Complete the coversheet honestly — "NO", or the Level 2 "YES" with its five confirmations. [S7]
10. **Keep one evidence folder for the unit:** searches, drafts, versions, prompt histories, and Grammarly outputs for both assessments; the declarations give the University a standing right to request them. [S3][S5][S7]

## Common confusions

- **Scholarly versus peer-reviewed.** They are not the same test: all peer-reviewed sources are scholarly, but a scholarly source such as an academic book may not have undergone peer review, so it is usable while "peer-reviewed" remains the stronger label. [S5]
- **Local time versus the listed deadline.** The verbal deadline reads in NSW time, but the operative rule is the portal's converted local display — a student in Queensland facing "11:59 pm AEST" submits by 10:59 pm local. [S5]
- **Grammarly as an invisible helper.** It is classified here as a GenAI tool: its paraphrasing must be referenced, usage recorded via the Tracking Grammarly template, and use declared on the coversheet. [S5][S6]
- **"NO" GenAI does not waive record-keeping.** Even under "NO", the coversheet assures that copies or screenshots of internet and library searches and versions of your work are kept and producible on request. [S5]
- **The Assessment 2 conclusion is a summary, not another reflection.** The brief limits it to key arguments and recommendations with no new information; new evidence or themes there deviate from the stated structure. [S7]
- **Twelve sources is a floor, not a negotiating target.** The brief requires evidence from at least 12 scholarly sources across sections covering concepts, colonisation, socioeconomic determinants, and primary health care. [S7]
- **Word-count boundaries.** The 1,000- and 2,200-word limits exclude the coversheet, which sits in the document but is not counted. [S5][S7]
- **Connection versus four separate answers.** Per the brief's Step 4 formulation, A1's four required elements must be connected in one explanation; four independent sections omit the interconnection the task grades. [S5]
