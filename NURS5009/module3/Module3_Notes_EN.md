## 1. Adolescent Development

Adolescence is a period of rapid physical, emotional, social and cognitive change. Development
is shaped by puberty, identity formation, peer influence, family context, culture and health
status.

**Key developmental tasks:**
- Identity formation
- Increased independence
- Peer belonging and social roles
- Risk-taking and experimentation
- Emotional regulation and decision-making

**Nursing implications:**
- Build rapport and trust
- Avoid assuming developmental stage from age alone
- Ask open questions and give time for disclosure
- Include the young person in decisions where appropriate

---

## 2. Sexual Orientation and Gender Identity

Young people may identify across diverse gender and sexuality spectrums. Nurses should avoid
assumptions and use inclusive language.

### Key concepts
- Sexual orientation: pattern of emotional and physical attraction
- Gender identity: a person’s internal sense of being male, female, non-binary, etc.
- Pronouns: words used to describe a person’s gender identity (e.g., she/her, he/him, they/them)

### Nursing practice
- Use the person’s preferred name and pronouns
- Ask respectfully about identity when relevant
- Avoid heteronormative or gendered assumptions
- Reflect on bias and cultural safety

---

## 3. Sexual Health and Health Education

Sexual health includes physical, emotional, mental and social wellbeing in relation to sexuality.
It involves safe relationships, consent, contraception, protection from sexually transmitted
infections, and respectful communication.

### Topics to discuss with young people
- Consent and boundaries
- Relationships and communication
- Contraception and pregnancy prevention
- STI prevention and testing
- Sexual wellbeing and safety

### Nursing approach
- Provide accurate, age-appropriate information
- Respect confidentiality while following mandatory reporting and safety guidelines
- Offer practical support and referral

---

## 4. Substance Use and Harm Reduction

Adolescents may use substances for curiosity, coping, stress relief, peer acceptance or social
connection. Use can range from experimentation to dependence.

### Common risk factors
- Peer pressure
- Mental health distress
- Trauma or violence
- Family disruption
- Social isolation

### Nursing response
- Use non-judgemental screening
- Ask about frequency, type, route, and context of use
- Assess for related risks: mental health, injury, violence, unsafe sex, legal issues
- Link to education, counselling and support services

---

## 5. Mental Health Assessment in Adolescence

Adolescents may present with anxiety, depression, stress, self-harm risk, sleep problems or
behavioural changes. Comprehensive assessment should include risk, protective factors and context.

### Common health indicators
- Mood changes
- Withdrawal or social isolation
- irritability or aggression
- Sleep and appetite changes
- Poor concentration
- Substance use
- Self-harm thoughts or behaviours

### Priority nursing actions
- Complete a respectful history and risk assessment
- Screen for suicidality and self-harm risk
- Observe behaviour, affect and communication
- Notice family or school stressors
- Escalate urgent concerns promptly

---

## 6. HEEADSSS Framework

HEEADSSS is a psychosocial assessment framework used with adolescents and young people.
It covers key areas of life and functioning.

**H**ome and environment
**E**ducation and employment
**E**ating
**A**ctivities
**D**rugs
**S**exuality
**S**uicide / depression
**S**afety

### Why it matters

This framework supports a structured, patient-centred conversation and helps identify risk,
strengths and social determinants affecting health.

---

## 7. Nutrition in Adolescence

Adolescents have high nutritional needs due to growth, hormonal changes and activity levels.
Poor nutrition can affect physical development, mood, concentration and long-term health.

### Common issues
- Inadequate intake or poor-quality diet
- Eating disorders
- Restricted dieting
- Overreliance on fast food or sugary drinks
- Nutritional deficiencies

### Nursing considerations
- Ask about eating patterns, food access, allergies and cultural practices
- Explore weight, growth and energy levels
- Provide health education and referral where needed
- Screen for signs of malnutrition or disordered eating

---

## 8. Professional Communication and Safety

Nurses caring for adolescents must balance confidentiality with safety. Young people need to feel
safe, heard and respected while being informed about limits of confidentiality.

### Good practice
- Explain confidentiality clearly
- Encourage honest disclosure without judgement
- Support consent and decision-making at developmentally appropriate levels
- Work with family and services when indicated

---

## 9. Key Takeaways

- Adolescent health is shaped by development, identity, social context and risk factors.
- Inclusive, respectful communication is essential.
- Sexual health and mental health should be addressed without stigma.
- Structured assessment tools such as HEEADSSS help identify needs and risks early.
- Nurse practice should be strengths-based, person-centred and trauma-informed.
