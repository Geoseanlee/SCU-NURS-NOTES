Module overview
-
-
Adolescence from ages 10 to 19 is a unique phase of human development, bridging childhood and adulthood. Adolescents undergo rapid physical, cognitive, and psychosocial growth during this period, influencing their emotions, decision-making, and interactions with the world. To ensure their well-being, adolescents require access to essential information, including age-appropriate comprehensive sexuality education, opportunities to acquire life skills, equitable and effective health services, and safe and supportive environments. It is equally crucial to involve adolescents in designing and delivering interventions to improve their health. This module is designed to explore a wide range of critical health issues that significantly impact adolescent health and development.
By the end of this module, you will be able to unpack the pertinent adolescent health issues pertaining to:
- The milestones of physical development during the adolescent years.
- Aspects of sexuality and gender relevant to adolescent health.
- Substance use and abuse during adolescence.
- Sexual health and the awareness of sexually transmitted infections.
- Mental health and overall well-being among adolescents including nutritional deficits or disorders during the adolescent phase

===== FILE: 05 - Physical development milestones of the adolescent years.epub =====
Physical development milestones of the adolescent years
-
-
Puberty marks the initial stage of adolescence when sexual organs commence their growth and maturation. It's essential to recognise that the onset and duration of adolescence are culturally influenced to some extent. For instance, in Australia, adolescence can extend for 18 to 20 years in certain cultures. Typically, girls begin puberty between the ages of 10 and 14, while boys experience this transition from 12 to 16 years of age. During this phase, there is a significant acceleration in growth compared to the slow, steady growth observed during childhood (Berman et al., 2018).
Key Physical Changes During Adolescence
- Full height is typically not attained until age 20 to 24 years.
- Males experience a significant increase in muscle mass.
- Muscle mass in males becomes approximately two times greater than in females by age 17.
- Adult cardiovascular rhythms are achieved by age 16 years.
- Nutritional needs are more significant during adolescence than at other stages of life.
- The male basal metabolic rate is about 10% greater at adolescence's end than females.
- By 12, individuals usually have all permanent teeth except for second and third molars.
- Girls reach approximately 90% of their adult height and 50% of their adult weight by age 11.
- Boys reach around 80% of their adult height and 50% of their adult weight by age 12.
In Module 1, we explored Human Development Theories, including Erikson's theory. According to Erikson, adolescence is crucial for establishing a sense of self and identity. This period often involves role confusion stemming from dramatic physical changes, leading to identity crises and conflicts between conforming to societal expectations and peer influences.

===== FILE: 10 - Substance use.epub =====
Substance use
-
-
One of the most pressing issues that healthcare providers and caregivers encounter is substance use among adolescents. This topic will provide an introduction to the complexities and emerging issues surrounding substance use, abuse, and addiction in the adolescent population.
Substance use and abuse in adolescence
Adolescents are influenced by various external and internal factors that significantly shape their decisions regarding substance use. Healthcare professionals must be well-informed and empathetic when addressing these issues, considering contributors such as peer pressure, mental health, and environmental influences. Substance use in adolescence can encompass alcohol consumption, binge drinking, cigarettes, vaping (e-cigarettes), recreational cannabis use, or illicit drug use. The correlation between alcohol, cigarette, and illicit drug use is well-established. A wide array of resources is available to document and discuss adolescent substance use.
During adolescence, decision-making is linked to the brain's reward centre, primarily influenced by dopamine, a neurotransmitter that elicits feelings of happiness. Teens possess a higher number of dopamine receptors, rendering them more sensitive to rewards, such as compliments, than adults. Being in the company of friends intensifies this sensitivity, impacting adolescents' choices, both positively by encouraging new experiences and negatively, potentially leading to risky behaviours like drug and alcohol use.

VAPING (e-cigarettes)
An emerging public health concern is vaping. Vaping involves the use of electronic cigarettes, often referred to as "vapes." These battery-powered devices heat a liquid to create a vapour, which appears as a "smoky cloud" of liquid droplets. Inhaling this vapour into the lungs is what constitutes vaping.
According to the Alcohol and Drug Foundation, a significant number of young people report using e-cigarettes. Surveys in the United Kingdom and Australia revealed that approximately 11 to 14% of adolescents have experimented with vaping. What sets e-cigarettes apart are the appealing and diverse flavours available, leading to a common perception that they are "less harmful than traditional cigarettes." However, in reality, most e-cigarettes contain nicotine, akin to regular cigarettes.
Additionally, vapes may contain cannabis compounds like THC and CBD, similar to a watered-down joint. These highly addictive substances provide a quick rush to teenagers but can be detrimental to the developing adolescent brain, negatively impacting functions such as learning, memory, and impulse control (Wong & Shipton, 2022).
https://dontforgetthebubbles.com/vaping-a-30-second-high/
Why do teens like vaping so much?
Clever marketing has portrayed vapes as a fun, cool, safe and stylised alternative to traditional cigarette smoking.
They smell fruity and delicious, like lolly-flavoured watermelon, fruity papaya, mint or even waffle-flavoured. These are particularly appealing to young people.
Vapes lack the offensive smell that traditional cigarettes possess. With the added bonus of looking inconspicuous (e.g., like USB dongles or pens) and being easy to conceal in school bags or shirt pockets.
They are also much cheaper than traditional cigarettes. Unlike cigarettes which are more prevalent in lower socio-economic groups, vaping was associated with higher SES groups, according to UK data (McNeill et al, 2021).
Despite being marketed as safe, there is limited safety data. Instead, there is emerging data on the health risks associated – compounded by the unregulated nature of the product.

===== FILE: 19 - Nutritional deficits or disorders in adolescence.epub =====
Nutritional deficits or disorders in adolescence
-
-
Nutritional deficits or disorders are common in adolescence and the prevalence seems to be increasing.
A study by Mitchison et al (2019) cited a point prevalence of any eating disorder of 12.8% in boys and 32.9% in girls.
Eating disorders have the highest mortality rate of any psychiatric illness.
*Trigger warning* This material may be distressing to some students.
The most common presentations include:
- Anorexia Nervosa
- Bulimia Nervosa
- Orthorexia Nervosa
- Binge Eating Disorder
- ARFID (Avoidant/ Restrictive Food Intake Disorder)
Adolescents with eating disorders (EDs) can present medically unstable with cardiovascular complications, electrolyte imbalances, and dehydration requiring medical admission.
There may be associated suicidal ideation, escalating ED compensatory behaviours (eg prolonged fasting) or comorbid psychiatric disorders requiring urgent mental health assessment (RCH,2017).
Registered nurses should obtain the following data in an acute presentation to the Emergency Department of Health facility:
- Lying & standing BP
- HR
- ECG
- Weight, Height and calculate BMI
- Baseline bloods
