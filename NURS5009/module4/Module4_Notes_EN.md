## 1. Ageing and Healthy Ageing

Ageing is a normal life process shaped by biology, environment, lifestyle, culture and social
condition. Healthy ageing is not simply being disease-free; it refers to maintaining functional
ability, resilience, participation and quality of life.

### Healthy ageing includes:
- Social participation
- Physical and mental wellbeing
- Independence and autonomy
- Safe environments and supportive communities
- Access to health services and social connection

### Important concept

Ageing is diverse. Not all older adults age in the same way; experience depends on health,
history, circumstances, access to care and personal values.

---

## 2. The Science of Ageing

Biologically, ageing includes gradual loss of physiological reserve and cellular function.
Examples include:
- Reduced skin elasticity and wound healing
- Lower muscle mass and strength
- Slower metabolism and energy reserve
- Changes in sensory function
- Changes in immune responses

These changes are often normal but may increase vulnerability to illness, injury, medication
side effects and poor recovery.

---

## 3. Midlife Development and Theories

### Levinson's theory of development

Levinson described adulthood as involving major transitions and life structures. Midlife is a
period of reassessment, meaning-making and sometimes tension between growth and decline.

### Erikson's psychosocial stages

Erikson’s theory highlights psychosocial development across the lifespan. In midlife, the key
developmental task is often related to generativity and stagnation.

- Generativity: contributing to family, work, community, or society
- Stagnation: feeling unproductive, unfulfilled or disconnected

### Nursing relevance

Midlife adults may face changes in identity, family roles, caring responsibilities, work,
physical capacity and expectations. Nurses should assess not only physical health but also life
stress, role burden and meaning.

---

## 4. Physical Assessment in Middle Adulthood

Middle-aged adults often experience gradual changes in body systems. Assessment should be
systematic and individualised.

### Common physical considerations
- Cardiovascular and metabolic changes
- Fatigue and reduced exercise tolerance
- Changes in mobility and strength
- Altered sensory function
- Sleep disruption
- Weight gain or loss
- Chronic conditions and medication burden

### Nursing practice
- Use holistic assessment and know baseline patterns
- Look for signs of deterioration or chronic disease burden
- Assess function, independence and safety
- Consider social and mental wellbeing alongside physical findings

---

## 5. Psychosocial Development in Midlife

Midlife may include challenges such as:
- Career pressures
- Caring for children and ageing parents
- Relationship changes
- Financial stress
- Loss and grief
- Identity shifts
- Body image or role strain

People may experience both growth and decline at the same time. Psychosocial health is not a
separate issue from physical health; they interact directly.

---

## 6. Internal and External Stressors

Stressors can be internal or external.

**Internal stressors:**
- Fear, anxiety, self-belief, illness perception, coping patterns

**External stressors:**
- Workload, financial pressures, relationship conflict, disability, caregiving, family illness,
  environmental challenges

### Nursing response
- Complete a broad assessment of stress and support systems
- Identify protective factors and coping strategies
- Refer for appropriate support where needed

---

## 7. Ageism and Bias

Ageism refers to stereotypes, prejudice and discrimination based on age. It can affect the way
older adults are treated, how much information is shared with them, and the quality of care they
receive.

### Effects of ageism
- Reduced dignity and respect
- Patience and information may be reduced
- Lower expectations for participation or recovery
- Negative self-perception and internalised ageism
- Poorer health outcomes and reduced quality of life

### Nursing responsibility
- Challenge assumptions about older adults
- Use respectful communication
- Avoid infantilising language or behaviour
- Value older adults as active participants in care

---

## 8. Domestic Violence and Community Safety

Domestic and family violence can affect people across the lifespan, including middle-aged and
older adults. Nurses must be alert to signs that may include fear, withdrawal, unexplained
injuries, control by another person, social isolation and reluctance to discuss private matters.

### Nursing responsibilities
- Ask about safety in a private, respectful way
- Assess immediate risk
- Provide information on support services and referral pathways
- Document clearly and follow safeguarding procedures
- Maintain confidentiality unless required by law or patient safety needs

---

## 9. Older Adult Rights and Person-Centred Care

Nursing care should uphold dignity, respect, autonomy and inclusion. This includes recognising
that older adults are individuals with agency, history and values.

### Good practice includes
- Listening without judgement
- Communicating clearly and respectfully
- Supporting independence where possible
- Encouraging social connection and meaningful activity
- Seeing the person beyond age or diagnosis

---

## 10. Key Takeaways

- Midlife and ageing are shaped by biology, culture, environment and personal meaning.
- Healthy ageing is about function, participation and wellbeing, not only the absence of disease.
- Ageism harms care and health outcomes.
- Nurses must assess physical, social and emotional well-being together.
- Screening for risk, including domestic violence, is part of safe and ethical nursing care.
