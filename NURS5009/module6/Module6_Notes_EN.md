## 1. Mental Health Nursing

Mental health is broader than the absence of mental illness. It includes emotional, social and
psychological wellbeing and functioning. Mental illness can affect thinking, mood, perception,
behaviour and capacity to function.

### Important ideas
- Mental health is dynamic and shaped by individual, interpersonal and social factors.
- Mental illness is not solely an individual problem; systems, relationships and context matter.
- Recovery-oriented care values hope, connection, strengths and participation.

### Nursing response
- Build therapeutic presence
- Avoid stigma or judgment
- Consider physical and mental health together
- Use psychosocial interventions as part of care

---

## 2. Psychosocial Interventions

Psychosocial interventions are nursing activities that support a person’s psychological and social
functioning. They may include communication, validation, emotional support, grounding, routine
building, therapeutic engagement and practical problem solving.

### Purpose
- improve coping
- strengthen communication and relationships
- reduce distress
- promote recovery and participation

### Factors that may interfere with good mental health care
- containment over recovery
- inadequate psychosocial intervention
- poor communication
- lack of time or resources
- fragmented or task-focused practice

---

## 3. Lived Experience and Narrative

Personal stories are powerful in mental health care. They help nurses understand: 
- what the person is experiencing
- what recovery means to them
- how stigma, trauma and support systems affect wellbeing

Hearing lived experience also reminds nurses that mental health care should be human, relational,
and person-centred.

---

## 4. Neurodiversity

Neurodiversity recognises that people think and process information differently. Autism is one
example of neurodivergent development.

### Key points
- Neurodiversity is not simply “difference” in a neutral sense; it may involve functional challenges
  in communication, sensory processing, social interpretation or adapting to expectation.
- Autism can affect communication, social understanding, routines, sensory experience and coping.
- People may require supports and reasonable adjustments rather than deficit-only approaches.

### Nursing focus
- Learn the person’s communication style
- Recognise sensory sensitivities and overwhelm
- Reduce unnecessary stress and uncertainty
- Avoid assumptions based on “typical” behaviour

---

## 5. Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD)

ASD is a lifelong neurodevelopmental condition that affects behaviour, communication and social
interaction. It can present differently in each person.

### Common considerations
- communication differences
- sensory sensitivity
- need for routine and predictability
- difficulty with abstract meaning or implicit social cues
- risk of distress when routines or expectations change

### Nursing care
- explain clearly and simply
- prepare before changes or procedures
- allow time and reduce sensory overload
- offer choices when possible
- communicate with family/carers when appropriate

---

## 6. Intellectual Disability

Intellectual disability is associated with differences in learning, processing speed, adaptive
functioning and support needs. It is not the same as neurodivergent style of thinking.

### Nursing considerations
- use clear, concrete communication
- allow extra processing time
- support participation and decision-making at the person's level
- minimise overwhelm and confusion
- maintain dignity and respect for individual capacity

---

## 7. Reasonable Adjustments

Reasonable adjustments are changes to systems, communication or environment that reduce barriers.
They are central to inclusion and person-centred care.

### Examples
- explain information in plain language
- provide visual supports or written instructions
- reduce noise and sensory load
- allow extra time for response
- adapt trust-building and communication strategies
- avoid punitive responses to communication differences

### Why they matter

People with ASD or intellectual disability often experience health inequalities because health
systems are designed around typical communication and processing patterns.

---

## 8. Positive Behaviour Support (PBS)

Positive behaviour support is a strengths-based approach. It looks at behaviour as having a
function, and it asks: what is the person trying to communicate or achieve?

### Principles
- behaviour is not random; it has meaning and function
- focus on understanding triggers, needs and supports
- use proactive strategies instead of reactive punishment
- build routines and communication supports
- strengthen adaptive skills and emotional regulation

### Nursing approach
- observe patterns
- identify antecedents, triggers and outcomes
- support communication and self-regulation
- collaborate with the person and family/carers

---

## 9. Rights, Inclusion and Advocacy

People with developmental disability or neurodivergence have rights to dignity, inclusion,
respect, participation, safety and healthcare access.

### Nursing responsibilities
- advocate for access and reasonable adjustments
- challenge stigma and exclusion
- ensure person-centred communication
- support safe and respectful healthcare experiences

---

## 10. Key Takeaways

- Mental health care must consider the whole person, not just diagnosis.
- Neurodiversity and disability are not deficits alone; they require thoughtful, inclusive supports.
- Reasonable adjustments are essential to safe and equitable care.
- Positive behaviour support focuses on understanding function and building strengths.
- Nurses play a central role in respectful, rights-based care.
