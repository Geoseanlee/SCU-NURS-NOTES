Understanding atherosclerosis
-
-
Introduction to Atherosclerosis
'Atherosclerosis' is a strange word,what does it mean? Well, 'sclero' comes from the Greek sklērós, meaning “hard.” Atherosclerosis therefore literally means hardening of the arteries.
It is a chronic inflammatory condition that results in fibrosis, scarring and narrowing of the arteries. But more importantly,   atherosclerosis is the underlying cause of about 50% of all deaths in westernized society.   Therefore we should take the time to learn as much about the disorder as possible because many of our therapeutic agents will target the risk factors of the disorder that allow the problem to progress.

===== FILE: 08 - HDL levels.epub =====
HDL levels
-
-
Dyslipidaemia and HDL
Recall that we use the term dyslipidaemia for individuals who have low levels of HDL, the good protein, because that is a risk factor for developing atherosclerosis. HDL is the ‘bus’ that carries cholesterol from the tissues back to the liver, so a low amount of the HDL will yield high amounts near the blood vessels. Nicotinic acid raises the HDL levels, but if that is not able to be tolerated, fenofibrate or other fibrates can be helpful. It is one of the few times that you will still see those medications still used.

Hypertension Pharmacology made simple: The ABCD framework
When we're thinking about the drugs that are used to treat hypertension, one of the ways in which we can easily remember those drugs is to simply remember the first four letters of the alphabet. We have spoken about a few of these medications already, and the next few lessons will bring it all together for you. Remember, A, B, C and D:
- Alpha 1 blockers (…azosin drugs)
- Alpha 2 agonists (yes, agonists,
- Angiotensin converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors
- Angiotensin 2 receptor blockers (ARBS; …sartan)
- Beta blockers
- Calcium channel blockers (Dihydropyridines)
- Diuretics – Especially Hydrochlorothiazide or thiazide diuretics
So, with respect to the alpha blockers, they are adrenergic antagonists that selectively affect the peripheral blood vessels.
The drugs of the renin cascade, being the angiotensin converting enzyme (ace inhibitors) and angiotensin 2 receptor blockers (ARBS),
The beta blockers that block the adrenaline binding to the heart
The calcium channel blockers that do not allow the calcium into the cells of the heart (calcium is needed for contraction)
And the diuretics, which we will discuss in the last lesson of this module.

===== FILE: 22 - Arterial clots.epub =====
Arterial clots
-
-
From haemostasis to thrombosis: mechanisms and consequences
Haemostasis is the process of stopping bleeding. We use it every day to repair small bleeds, and it is vital to our survival. But our lifestyle has lead to us being predisposed to this process occurring in absence of a break in the blood vessel walls. In that case, a thrombus develops. Thrombi can occlude the site in which it develops or they can break off from the original site of formation to a blood vessel downstream. In order to combat the development of thrombi, we target the same process in which they develop, the platelet plug, the clotting cascade and the natural dissolving of the clot. ===== FILE: 23 - Antiplatelet drugs.epub =====
Antiplatelet drugs
-
-
The role of platelets in haemostasis and drug targeting
Platelets are invaluable in the process of haemostasis, and as such, most of our antiplatelet drugs will not disable the platelet altogether, but rather just take away one of the platelet's 'tools'. In order to understand our antiplatelet drugs, the most important thing to remember is that platelets are not cells, and as such, they do not contain the DNA to synthesise more 'tools'. So when we take away one of the platelet's tools that allows for optimal aggregation, that 'tool' will be disabled for the entire life of the platelet, which is 9 days.

===== FILE: 29 - Understanding diuretics 2.epub =====
Understanding diuretics 2
-
-
The role of diuretics in heart failure and hypertension
The main indications for the use of diuretics are congestive heart failure and hypertension. In CHF, there is additional fluid being forced into the interstitial spaces due to increased back pressure. Relieving some of that fluid is beneficial as the heart can work more efficiently. In hypertension, recall that blood pressure is partly dependent on the amount of fluid, and as such, a reduction of fluid is likely to reduce BP - not dramatically, but reductions of 6-12 mmHg are common. ===== FILE: 30 - Thiazide diuretics.epub =====
Thiazide diuretics
-
-
Overview of Thiazide diuretics
The Thiazide and thiazide like diuretics are the most widely used diuretics. They are often combined with other diuretics to increase the efficacy and decrease the side effects. For instance, thiazide diuretics waste potassium. But if a small amount of thiazide diuretics is added along with a potassium sparing diuretic or to ACE inhibitors, or angiotensin receptor blockers, the potassium levels often normalise and the diuresis increases. ===== FILE: 32 - Potassium sparing diuretics (Not compulsory).epub =====
Potassium sparing diuretics (Not compulsory)
-
-
Spironolactone and aldosterone receptor blockade
The material in this lesson will not be assessed in PHAR2001.
Aldosterone is a mineralocorticoid that is released from the adrenal cortex and is nicknamed the salt saver. It saves salt by binding to the cells in the distal convoluted tubule.
Spironolactone binds to the aldosterone receptors and blocks those receptors, preventing the salt saving actions. It also has antiandrogenic actions, blocking some of the testosterone receptors.
-   Although spironolactone is not as effective in reducing deaths in those individuals with CHF as compared to Loop diuretics, it is still highly rated for that.
-   For hypertension, there are normally better diuretics, but in the subset of hypertensive individuals who have excess aldosterone (1% of the population), the medication is very effective.
