In today's lecture, we delved into the pharmacological agents used in the treatment of dyslipidemia or hyperlipidemia, with a focus on the HMG coenzyme A reductase inhibitors, commonly known as statins. We learned that statins work by inhibiting the enzyme responsible for cholesterol synthesis in the liver, thereby decreasing the production of the "bad" LDL cholesterol. Statins are particularly effective in treating familial hypercholesterolemia, a genetic disorder characterized by high LDL levels. Interestingly, statins have been found to provide additional benefits beyond their lipid-lowering effects, such as anti-inflammatory, antithrombotic, and atherosclerotic plaque-stabilizing properties, which contribute to their positive impact on mortality, longevity, and the prevention of thrombotic events. Additionally, statins are contraindicated in pregnancy and liver disease, and it is important to monitor liver enzymes in patients taking these drugs. The lecture also highlighted the variability in the effects of different statins, emphasizing the need to stay informed about new and potentially more beneficial statins that may become available in the future.
