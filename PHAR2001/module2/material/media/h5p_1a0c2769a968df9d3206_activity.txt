Labetalol is a valuable antihypertensive agent that is frequently used in women with pregnancy-induced hypertension, including pre-eclampsia. It is a pregnancy category C drug, which means that it could possibly cause harm to the foetus but if pre-eclampsia advances to eclampsia, it becomes a life threatening condition for both mother and child. Labetalol can be considered a non-selective alpha blocker or a non-selective beta blocker owing to the fact that it has cross over effects onto both Alpha 1 and Beta 1 receptors. This gets confusing, as Beta 1 blockers will normally end in the suffix OLOL. The thing to remember about labetalol is the fact that it blocks the action of adrenaline on the peripheral blood vessels, thus, in effect, dilating them and blocks the beta 1 receptors on the receptors of the heart, thus decreasing heart rate and contractility. This combination appears to be especially effective in treating eclampsia, which involves increased blood pressure and proteinuria during pregnancy.
