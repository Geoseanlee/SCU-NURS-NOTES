The objectives for this Chapter include:

Describe the actions, interactions and side effects of the following adrenergic agonists:

  • Adrenaline (non-selective agonist)
  • Phenylephrine (an alpha 1 selective agonist)
  • Dobutamine (a beta 1 adrenergic agonist)
  • Salbutamol (a beta 2 adrenergic agonist)
  • Dopamine

These medications will be taken again at a later time. It is most important right now to understand why the actions and side effects occur

     Alpha 1 agonists: side effects

     actions can be increased, but the most
     common adverse effects include:

       • Hypertension
       • Constipation
       • Urinary retention

       • (eye pain is possible)




23




                     Actions: Alpha 1 agonists
         • Nasal passages and eyes:
            • The constrictive effects of Alpha 1 adrenergic agonists can
              counteract the vasodilation that leads to nasal congestion and
              the ‘red eye’.
         • Systemically:
            • Increase in blood pressure (through a constriction of blood
              vessels).
         • Dilate the pupils
            • Allows as much light in as possible but can also lead to eye
              pain when too much light is being allowed in.
         • In minor surgery
            • Local administration constricts blood vessels for less bleeding
              and decreased systemic absorption of local anaesthesia

              Actions: Beta 1 adrenergic agonists
          • Positive chronotropic effects (increased heart rate)
          • Positive inotropic effect (increase in the contractile force of the
            heart)
             • Know these terms – chrono=time (beats/min) inos=fibre (how
               much stretch
          • Therefore these drugs are useful in circulatory shock,
            hypotension and cardiac arrest
          • Adverse effects
             • GIT – constipation
             • Kidneys – decreased blood flow = increased systemic blood
               pressure via the renin angiotensin aldosterone mechanism
          • Example: Dobutamine (know this drug; used to treat acute but
            potentially reversible heart failure)
