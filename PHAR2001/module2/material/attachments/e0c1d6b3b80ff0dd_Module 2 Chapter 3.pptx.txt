Objectives
Describe the actions interactions and side effects of the following adrenergic antagonists:
Phentolamine and labetalol (non-selective antagonist)
Prazosin and doxazosin (an alpha 1 selective antagonist)
Metoprolol and atenolol (a beta 1 selective adrenergic antagonist)
These medications will be taken again at a later time. It is most important right now to understand why the actions and side effects occur.

## ppt/slides/slide7.xml
Non-selective sympatholytic drugs
Mostly used to treat hypertension
Labetalol (know this drug; used to treat pre-eclampsia)
Adverse effects
Postural hypotension 
Blurred vision
Bronchospasm (dyspnoea; contraindicated in asthma)
Swollen ankles
Nasal congestion

## ppt/slides/slide9.xml
Alpha antagonists 
Recall the location of receptors:
Blood vessels 
Smooth muscles of the GIT
Radial muscles of the eye
Sphincters of the bladder
If you block the adrenergic activity of these, 
Vasodilation = decreased blood pressure
Increased GI motility and secretions
Pupil constriction
Increased micturition

## ppt/slides/slide17.xml
Beta 1 antagonists
The blockage of the beta 1 receptors in the heart 
Decreased chronotropic effects 
Decreased inotropic effects
Together, these have the effect of 
stabilising the rhythm and 
leading to decrease in arrhythmias, 
decreased cardiac workload 
decreased oxygen consumption
The blockage of the beta 1 receptors in the kidneys 
Renin- angiotensin-aldosterone mechanism decreased 
Decreased blood pressure

## ppt/slides/slide20.xml
There is no clinical use for Beta 2 antagonists in humans
Why?
Recall the location of the receptors:
Lung
Mast cells
Skeletal muscle
(These would constrict the bronchioles and make mast cells more sensitive, so there is no clinical application for this type of drug)
