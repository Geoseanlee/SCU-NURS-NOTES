                               Cholinergic agonists
             Direct acting non-selective agonists include:

                  • Pilocarpine (know this drug; used to treat glaucoma. While
                    this is a non-selective agonist, pilocarpine is administered
                    topically and has little systemic absorption, therefore the
                    actions are fairly limited to the m3 receptors in the eyes)

                  • Bethanechol (know this drug; previously used for non-
                    obstructive urinary retention. Adverse effects:
                    bradycardia, hypotension, diarrhoea, all consistent with
                    parasympathetic stimulation.

          Indirect acting cholinergic agonists
     • Act on acetylcholinesterase at the post synaptic cleft
       • Prevents the breakdown of acetylcholine
          • More acetylcholine remains longer
       • Drugs cause a reversible bond; nerve gas is irreversible
     • Used for patients with:
       • Myasthenia gravis
          • (Edrophonium is used to diagnose myasthenia gravis)
          • Neostigmine (know this drug) and pyridostigmine are used to treat MG
       • Alzheimer’s disease
          • Donepezil (know this drug)
          • Galantamine (know this drug)
          • Rivastigmine

                          Advanced Alzheimer’s disease
             • Donepezil (Aricept; know this drug)
                • Reversible acetylcholinesterase inhibitor that is
                  100% bioavailable and crosses the BB barrier
                • Two clinical trials indicate it may be effective for
                  moderate to severe Alzheimer’s disease
             • Should be used with caution in patients with cardiac
               disease, cardiac conduction disturbances, chronic
               obstructive pulmonary disease, asthma
             • Patients with gastrointestinal disorders should use
               caution because nausea or vomiting may occur.

37




            This is a common mnemonic for
               anticholinergic overdose
     To describe the physiologic manifestations of atropine overdose:

       “Hot as a hare, blind as a bat, dry as a bone, red as a beet, and mad
                              as a hatter“ describing
     • warm, dry skin from decreased sweating,
     • blurry vision, decreased lacrimation,
     • vasodilation, and
     • central nervous system effects on muscarinic receptors

     This set of symptoms is known as anticholinergic toxidrome, and
     may also be caused by other drugs with anticholinergic effects, such
     as hyoscine hydrobromide (scopolamine), diphenhydramine,
     phenothiazine antipsychotics and benztropine
