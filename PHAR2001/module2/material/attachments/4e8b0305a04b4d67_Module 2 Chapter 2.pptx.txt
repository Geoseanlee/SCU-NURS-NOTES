Smooth muscles of the GIT (less effect than Beta 1 stimulation)
Sphincters of the bladder
Radial muscles of the eye

## ppt/slides/slide23.xml
Alpha 1 agonists: side effects
Any sympathetic nervous system actions can be increased, but the most common adverse effects include: 
Hypertension
Constipation 
Urinary retention
(eye pain is possible)

## ppt/slides/slide24.xml
Actions: Alpha 1 agonists
Nasal passages and eyes:
The constrictive effects of Alpha 1 adrenergic agonists can counteract the vasodilation that leads to nasal congestion and the ‘red eye’.
Systemically:
Increase in blood pressure (through a constriction of blood vessels).
Dilate the pupils
Allows as much light in as possible but can also lead to eye pain when too much light is being allowed in. 
In minor surgery	
Local administration constricts blood vessels for less bleeding and decreased systemic absorption of local anaesthesia

## ppt/slides/slide26.xml
Alpha 2 agonists
This is the exception to the rule so ignore for now. 
Explanation: the Alpha 2 receptor exists to make sure that the adrenaline response does not result in so much stimulation that it can do harm. It is a ‘negative feedback’ type of receptor and does exactly opposite to that which one would expect.
