Adrenaline and pseudoephedrine

Aside from adrenaline itself, the non-selective adrenergic agonists also include pseudoephedrine, which is a very effective decongestant and dopamine, which is pretty much only used in acute heart failure where the client needs a lot of stimulation to the heart in order to survive. We briefly look at both of those in this lesson.

It is an oral decongestant that is very similar to phenylephrine (another decongestant)

It causes a constriction of the ‘peripheral’ blood vessels, which is the reason it is effective but that can result in an increase in blood pressure

It can result in an increase in heart rate, as there is some cross over action on the Beta 1 receptor, especially in higher doses.

It may be associated with dilated pupils because it acts like adrenaline on the alpha 1 receptor

It is a strong stimulant
