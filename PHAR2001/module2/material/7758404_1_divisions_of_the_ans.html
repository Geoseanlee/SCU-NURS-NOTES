The two divisions of the ANS

The two divisions of the autonomic nervous system are the Sympathetic Nervous System (SNS) and the Parasympathetic Nervous System (PNS). The SNS and PNS have, for the most part, opposite effects because the autonomic nervous system helps maintain homeostasis. So, if our heart was beating too slow for the conditions at the time, our cells would start getting too little oxygen, lactic acid would start to accumulate and homeostasis would be disrupted. But that does not happen because the SNS comes to the rescue, sending out a bit of adrenaline and homeostasis is maintained.

For the most part, we can predict what will happen with a sympathetic response if we take each organ and think about it logically. The exception to that is the effect of that response in the urinary system. With a fight or flight response, there are things relaxing and contracting all over the place, but the ultimate response is that "we need to flee not wee". There is an overall decrease in the ability to urinate. (But in some cases, you will hear about someone who did the opposite during a good fright, because of the complexity of the response at the urinary system.)
