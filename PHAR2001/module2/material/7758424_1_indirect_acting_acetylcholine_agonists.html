Understanding Indirect acting acetylcholine agonists

The most common type of nerve gas irreversibly inhibits the enzyme acetylcholinesterase. Think about what that would do. There would be huge amounts of acetylcholine in the synaptic cleft because it is not degrading. But where would that excess be? In the MUSCLES, meaning there would be constant contraction - how can you breath if the diaphram is not able to relax? In the HEART, meaning there would be a profound bradycardia (decreased heart rate). In the GIT, giving excess salivation, cramping and diarrhoea. Now lets back up a bit and think about when a reversible version of that could be useful.
