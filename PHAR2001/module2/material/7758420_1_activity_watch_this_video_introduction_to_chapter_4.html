Objectives, PowerPoint and Notes

The objectives for Chapter 4 include:

  • Explain the difference between direct-acting and indirect-acting Parasympathomimetic drugs
  • Know terms used to describe the parasympathomimetic drugs, for instance, muscarinic drugs, cholinergic agents, acetylcholine agonists.
  • Describe the actions interactions and side effects of the:
    • Direct acting cholinergic agonists bethanechol and pilocarpine
    • Indirect acting cholinergic agonists neostigmine and donepezil
    • The antimuscarinic agents, using atropine as an example.

Cholinergic agonists
Direct acting non-selective agonists include:
Pilocarpine 
(know this drug; used to treat glaucoma. While this is a non-selective agonist, pilocarpine is administered topically and has little systemic absorption, therefore the actions are fairly limited to the m3 receptors in the eyes)
Bethanechol
 (know this drug; previously used for non-obstructive urinary retention. Adverse effects: bradycardia, hypotension, diarrhoea, all consistent with parasympathetic stimulation.

## ppt/slides/slide31.xml
Advanced Alzheimer’s disease
Donepezil
 (Aricept; know this drug) 
Reversible acetylcholinesterase inhibitor that is 100% bioavailable and crosses the BB barrier
Two clinical trials indicate it may be effective for moderate to severe Alzheimer’s disease
Should be used with caution in patients with cardiac disease, cardiac conduction disturbances, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, asthma
Patients with gastrointestinal disorders should use caution because nausea or vomiting may occur.

## ppt/slides/slide38.xml
This is a common mnemonic for anticholinergic overdose
To describe the physiologic manifestations of atropine overdose: 
“Hot as a hare, blind as a bat, dry as a bone, red as a beet, and mad as a hatter“ describing 
warm, dry skin from decreased sweating, 
blurry vision, decreased lacrimation, 
vasodilation, and 
central nervous system effects on muscarinic receptors
This set of symptoms is known as anticholinergic toxidrome, and may also be caused by other drugs with anticholinergic effects, such as hyoscine hydrobromide (scopolamine), diphenhydramine, phenothiazine antipsychotics and benztropine
