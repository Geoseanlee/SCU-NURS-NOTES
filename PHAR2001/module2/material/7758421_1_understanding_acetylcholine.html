Learning about acetlycholine

Recall that the neurotransmitter that is used at the neuromuscular junction that results in a contraction of the muscle is acetylcholine. And now we are saying that the neurotransmitter that works at the effector (at each of the organs of the ANS) is acetylcholine. 

  • So how is it that we can have medications that affect our organs without affecting muscle movement?

In this lesson, you will learn the answer to that question, and also learn the different names used for Parasympathetic Nervous System (PNS) drugs, and what their general effect will be if they cross into the brain.

The acetylcholine that is released by the motor nerves at the neuromuscular junction binds to NICOTINIC receptors. (In other words, the neurotransmitter that moves your muscles binds to nicotinic receptors)

The acetylcholine that is released at the effector in the parasympathetic nervous system (PNS), binds to MUSCARINIC receptors. (In other words, the main receptors for Acetylcholine in the PNS are the muscarinic receptors.)

When we give a drug that affects the Parasympathetic Nervous System, it always affects muscle movement.
