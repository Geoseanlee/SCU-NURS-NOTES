Types of antacids

There are four types of antacids that are frequently used in Australia. But how do you know which is best for the individual? For instance, if client A is normally a bit constipated, they would do best by taking the magnesium antacids, and if client B generally has softer stools, they may do better with the aluminium antacids. This lesson gives you a clear picture of the strengths and weaknesses of all four antacids so that you can decide which, if any, is best for the individual client.

Which of the following antacids is/are ABSORBED, potentially resulting in greater side effects?

Untitled Multiple Choice

Magnesium hydroxide is an antacid that is osmotically active and not absorbed. As such it causes diarrhoea in many people.

Aluminium hydroxide or citrate is an antacid that causes constipation and also binds to many drugs and nutrients so care should be taken not to take them with meals or medications.

Calcium carbonate has the strongest antacid effects but also has the strongest rebound hyperacidity

Sodium bicarbonate is the preferred antacid because it is not absorbed
