Stimulant laxatives

If you have ever had a GIT 'bug' and ended up going to the toilet about 20 times, you may have wondered why that was happening. Well, the toxins from the bacteria and the bacteria themselves are basically working on the enteric nervous system of the GIT to signal an increased motility and increased secretions. We want to get those toxins out ASAP!

That is a simplistic picture of what the stimulant laxatives do. They irritate the GIT to really get the intestines moving. 

But think about that. It is really logical when you think about them, that chronic (long term) use of the stimulant laxatives would result in damage to the GIT. Find out more about the stimulant laxatives and the very short list of situations in which they are indicated by participating in this lesson. 

But wait, there is more! (there is always more in this module, so try not to skip anything). We talk about enteric coating, (why some drugs use an enteric coating, how to take drugs that are enteric coated, and what not to do with them) as well. 

In the above description of this lesson, I note a number of important points that you will learn by watching this 7 minute video and engaging in the activities.
