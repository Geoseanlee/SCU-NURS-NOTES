Understanding affinity, potency and selectivity

Why is it that medication A, which works in the same way as Medication B can be so much stronger (more potent)? While there can be many reasons, one of the most common has to do with the strength of the molecular bond of the medication to its given receptor.

Most often, a medication that affects cellular receptors binds to its receptor and then lets go shortly after. Then it binds to another receptor of the same type, and lets go... But what if the drug bound to the receptor and it really takes a lot more time to get it off that receptor (it had a much greater affinity to the receptor)? As you can imagine, many more messages will be sent to the cell. So the drug's affinity can affect its potency. 

This is another really important lesson as we learn three very important concepts, affinity, potency and selectivity.
