# PHAR2001 Introductory Pharmacology — Assessment Guide

## Overview

PHAR2001 Introductory Pharmacology in Term 5 2026 is assessed through exactly three individual tasks: an open-notes Blackboard quiz on the Module 1 foundations (20%), a workbook built from multi-part tutorial questions (30%), and a case study set in a multi-disciplinary clinic (50%). Over half of the final grade rests on written work requiring research beyond the learning materials. [R1][R4][R3] The Unit Assessor explains in the overview video that each assessment has its own folder under "Assessment Tasks and Submission" on Blackboard holding the brief, template, and submission link, and that every tutorial is designed to prepare you for one of these assessments. [V2][S6]

The tasks build one skill chain. The quiz checks that your foundational pharmacology is safe to practise on, because safety issues around specific medications and fatal drug interactions matter to every health-care worker. [R1] The workbook develops communicating accurate medication information to colleagues and clients. [R4] The case study adds the skill both briefs stress most: knowing where to look for accurate medication information so you keep working correctly after University. [R3]

## What you must be able to do

1. Explain the main principles of pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics (LO 1 / ULO 1), tested in all three tasks. [R1][R4][R3]
2. Describe the mechanism of action, pharmacological effects, therapeutic uses, contraindications, safety issues, and side effects of major drug classes and commonly used agents (LO 2 / ULO 2). [R1][R4][R3]
3. Discuss drug toxicity and interactions between drugs using specific examples and individual variability of response (ULO 3), assessed in the workbook and case study. [R4][R3]
4. Investigate and evaluate medications in case-based contexts and communicate findings accurately and timely (ULO 4), the case study's core demand. [R3]
5. Sit a controlled individual online quiz under strict conditions: your notes and Tutorial 1 websites only, no GenAI, no screenshots, no backtracking. [R1]
6. Write connected prose in tight word budgets — never point form — foregrounding safety, with APA 7th references for external information. [R2][R4]
7. Research with a maximum of two references per case-study answer, citing only original sources that directly support the claim, and never the modules or workshops. [R3]

## 2. Assessment 1: Week 2 quiz (20%)

### 2.1 Task and conditions

Assessment 1 is a Blackboard quiz of multiple choice and short answer questions, worth 20%, individual, 60 minutes, assessing LO 1 and LO 2. [R1] Because answers are simply correct or incorrect, the brief states there is no rubric. [R1] On entry you agree to all conditions, including completing the quiz on your own. [R1]

You may use your study notes and the websites discussed in Tutorial 1. [R1] You may not use ChatGPT or other AI sources, past PHAR2001 exams, or communicate with any person, and you may not take pictures, screenshots, or snips of the questions. [R1] GenAI level is Level 3: No GenAI Use, and you may be required to demonstrate non-use, so keep accurate records of your GenAI activity. [R1]

### 2.2 Question behaviour

Questions are randomised three ways: the order of questions, the order of the a–e options, and the selection itself — items come from a larger pool. [R1] No backtracking is allowed, so you cannot revise an earlier answer. [R1] The quiz must be completed in one sitting, and Blackboard auto-submits 60 minutes after entry. [R1]

If your connection fails, enter the quiz again using the same path; if re-entry fails, email your Unit Assessor, available 06:00 to 22:00 on quiz day. [R1] Bring a good connection plus backup plans for internet and computer failure — or sit the quiz at any SCU campus, where no backup plan is needed. [R1]

### 2.3 Preparation

The brief names the best preparation: the activities and videos from Module 1, since every quiz question is modelled on that module's activities, and attendance at the Week 2 Tutorial is necessary for full marks. [R1] The orientation lessons are explicitly assessable: scheduling of drugs, medicinal formulations, and pregnancy categories. [S8][S9][S10][S12] Read the feedback attached to activity answers, and note the scheduling correction that alprazolam (Xanax) and flunitrazepam are the only two benzodiazepines listed as Schedule 8 at this time. [S8]

Two structural facts shape your study. First, the Week 2 tutorial previews the question types with specific examples, and because tutorials are mandatory, some questions can only be answered from tutorial content — those answers are not in the slides and cannot be researched online. [V1] Second, quiz wording forces several look-ups per question, so consulting sources live is too slow; the orientation material instead advises building a folder of notes you can navigate quickly. [S11][S7] Its example question shows the pattern: a medicine the TGA requires a pharmacist to dispense but that needs no prescription is a Schedule 3 medicine. [S11]

### 2.4 Results

Feedback and grades are released Sunday 8 November, 11:59 pm AEST; allow up to seven days for posting. [R1] Because the result arrives before census date on Monday of Week 3, a disappointing quiz still leaves time to withdraw without penalty and resume in a later term, as the unit runs in Terms 1, 3, and 5. [V1][V2]

## 3. Assessment 2: Workbook (30%)

### 3.1 Task structure

Assessment 2 is worth 30% and built from two of three multi-part tutorial questions delivered in the Week 3, 4, and 5 tutorials. [R4] Question B (Week 4) is compulsory; you then answer Question A (Week 3) or Question C (Week 5). [R4][R2] Each answered question is worth 15 points, and every sub-question within your chosen questions must be answered. [R4] The questions exist only in the live tutorial and the video tutorial series on Blackboard — not posted elsewhere, and not available until the tutorial has been delivered. [R2]

| Question | Topic | Sub-question point split |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A (Week 3, optional) | Antihistamines | 5 / 3 / 3 / 4: a comparison across the tutorial's antihistamines; side effects; indications; and diphenhydramine |
| B (Week 4, compulsory) | Diabetes | 7 / 4 / 4: the most important contributing factors; gliclazide; metformin |
| C (Week 5, optional) | Depression and pain | 5 / 4 / 6: amitriptyline as the example; amitriptyline and other drugs; a question built on the opioid |

The template pre-fills the first few words of each sub-question so you can confirm you answer the right item; you type the rest of the question, then your answer in the box. [R2] Missed tutorials are posted on Blackboard Ultra. [R2]

### 3.2 Word limits and answer format

Each whole question, including all sub-parts, must fit 600 words; both questions together allow a maximum of 1200 words plus 10%; question text is excluded, and the distribution across sub-questions is your choice. [R4][R2] The costliest rule in the assessment: point form receives zero marks, because you do not speak to a client in point form — write in full sentences and paragraphs. [R2]

### 3.3 Safety emphasis and referencing by grade target

Safety is highly weighted: consider serious risks and risks with regular use, and emphasise them clearly. [R2] When a question asks for the "most important side effects", include both the most serious and the most commonly experienced. [R2] Answers must draw out the key points highlighted in the tutorials (Week 3 for A, Week 4 for B, Week 5 for C). [R2]

Referencing follows your grade target. Tutorial content alone suffices for a pass and needs no referencing. [R2][R4] Anything beyond the tutorial must be cited in APA 7th; above-pass marks require additional references guided by tutorial priorities, using the reliable resources identified in Tutorial 1; the interactive textbook in the unit content folder helps you understand the questions. [R2][R4]

### 3.4 Submission, declaration, and marking

Save the file as SurnameInitials_Workbook, enter your name and student number, and submit via the Turnitin point "Assessment 2: Workbook". [R2][R4] Font and spacing are unrestricted, but the format must suit Turnitin — Word (.doc/.docx), OpenOffice Text, WordPerfect, PostScript, HTML, and similar; no handwritten answers. [R4]

Two declarations apply. GenAI is Level 2: Purpose-Specific, limited to formatting, spelling, and grammar — Grammarly for language only. [R4][R2] You must also write an affirmation with your name and student number confirming you have read the SCU academic integrity policy and will not breach it, stating how you used AI; a workbook without it scores 0 out of 30. [R2]

Marking is two criteria worth 50% each: the first and the second tutorial question you answer. [R4] Bands: HD+ 100%, HD 85–99%, Distinction 75–84%, Credit 65–74%, Pass 50–64%, Marginal Fail 35–49%, Fail 1–34%, Not Attempted 0%. [R4] HD requires safety addressed correctly and comprehensively, no point form, no incomplete or incorrect answers, no omission of the tutorial-emphasised content, and appropriately referenced expansion from reliable resources. [R4] Credit allows missing tutorial-highlighted details or safety not fully addressed yet still safe; Pass may result simply from having no resources beyond the tutorial. [R4] Fail follows from unsafe recommendations, incoherent answers missing the question or the emphasised content, or unreferenced outside information. [R4] Zero comes from extensive plagiarism — the software detects students working together and rewriting portions — so complete the assignment entirely on your own. [R4] Feedback is released Wednesday 2 December, 11:59 pm NSW. [R4]

## 4. Assessment 3: Case study (50%)

### 4.1 Task structure

Assessment 3 is worth 50%, set in a multi-disciplinary clinic where you advise clients and their families. [R3][R5] You download the template "Assessment 3: Case study" and answer a five-part case correlating with Modules 2–6 on Blackboard. [R3] The sequence matters: study each module before its case part, because modules give background knowledge rather than exact answers; then research each question using the Tutorial 1 strategies. [R3] Every question carries its own weighting and suggested word count directly after the question. [R3]

| Part | System, week | Client scenario | Questions: weights and suggested words |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Respiratory, Week 2 | Quinn, 3, viral upper respiratory tract infection triggering an episodic asthma attack; salbutamol via metered-dose inhaler with mask and spacer; no antibiotic despite the mother's request; paracetamol for headache; prophylactic salbutamol at future infections' first signs | Why spacer with mask rather than inhaler alone (6%, ~70 words); signs of respiratory distress in a 3-year-old, 1% per clarified sign (6%, ~120); pharmacokinetics of orally consumed paracetamol covering absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, 1% per fact (8%, ~120) |
| B | Endocrine, Week 3 | FT, 29, Graves' disease: elevated free thyroxine, suppressed TSH, TSH-receptor antibodies, goitre; carbimazole plus metoprolol | Onset of carbimazole's clinical effects and the underlying reason (6%, ~60); red-flag advice before the first dose (7%, ~120); beta-blocker rationale, symptoms that improve, when to stop (7%, ~50) |
| C | Cardiovascular, Week 4 | Haemostasis framework: platelet aggregation, coagulation, fibrinolysis; each drug targets one process, since targeting two causes excess bleeding; TT in acute myocardial infarction; JD with arterial plaques, regular normal heartbeat; CH with pulmonary emboli; one drug per client, no drug reused | Who receives aspirin plus clopidogrel, and why combined (6%, ~50); who receives apixaban, and why preferred over warfarin (7%, ~60); who receives tenecteplase, and why preferred over streptokinase (7%, ~60) |
| D | Neurology, Week 5 | RR, 75, short-term memory problems on donepezil 10 mg at night; promethazine 10 mg at night for hay fever; diazepam 10 mg at night for five years; amitriptyline for depression | Donepezil's general mechanism and why it may help dementia (5%, ~70); why ceasing diazepam is best and how to cease it (7%, ~65); the problem with promethazine in this regimen, and antihistamine advice (8%, ~80) |
| E | Week 6 | TRM, 38, first IV dose of an oral penicillin course: within minutes, difficulty breathing, rapid heart rate, dizziness, blood pressure drop greater than 20 mmHg; judged a true IgE-mediated allergic reaction | Treatment of penicillin allergy by severity with rationale, including the treatment for the most extreme reaction (12%, ~200); whether this matches the hypersensitivity of a wrong-blood-type transfusion, and that reaction's treatment (8%, ~150) |

Each part's questions total 20% of the assessment, summing to the 50% unit weight. [R5][R3] The case supplies background you should act on: Graves' disease is autoimmune hyperthyroidism in which antibodies overstimulate the thyroid, producing excess T3 and T4, while thionamides such as carbimazole inhibit the enzyme needed for new thyroid hormone; the Week 3 workshop elaborates. [R5] The paracetamol question warns against details beyond unit scope — the volume of distribution or oxidation through the cytochrome CYP2E1 enzyme — and against listing facts you do not understand. [R5] The carbimazole red-flags question separates extremely rare effects (Stevens-Johnson syndrome, 2 cases; acute pancreatitis, 0.03%) from the two rare-but-more-frequent serious effects you should actually discuss. [R5]

### 4.2 Word guidance

Suggested words are not a limit: each figure is the length the Unit Assessor took for a model, full-credit answer. [R5][R3] The ceiling is twice the suggested count per question, where penalties apply; try not to exceed 50% more. [R3][R5] If far off either side, use the relevant week's workshop or discussion board to confirm what the question asks. [R5]

### 4.3 Referencing rules

For most questions you provide the single most beneficial resource, either APA 7th (SCU Library Guides give guidance) or a pasted URL — the choice does not affect your grade. [R3] Three hard limits: answers from AI sources such as ChatGPT or AI-based search results earn no credit; cited information must be directly supported by the referenced source, with no credit if the citation does not appear in it; and no more than two references per question. [R3]

Never reference the modules or workshops: they may contain answers, but the rationale is the skill of finding accurate information in post-University resources, so if you locate an answer in a workshop, keep researching until another resource carries it. [R3] Workshops and the discussion board may still give hints, and the unit content folder helps you understand the material you research. [R3]

### 4.4 Submission, declaration, and marking

Label the file SurnameInitials_Case Study and submit via the Turnitin point "Assessment 3: Case study". [R3] Font and spacing are unrestricted; formats match the workbook's Turnitin-acceptable list. [R3] Before answering, enter your name and student number, and add an affirmation of the SCU academic integrity policy; without it, 0 out of 50. [R5]

GenAI is Level 2: Purpose-Specific: search engines, even with AI-assisted features, may be used for preliminary information gathering, but AI-generated outputs must not be cited, must not form or redesign your answers; you must locate, read, and critically engage with the original literature and cite only original sources. [R3][R5] Grammarly is permitted for language improvement. [R3][R5]

Five criteria, one per part, each 20%; Parts A–D assess ULOs 1–4, Part E assesses ULOs 2 and 4. [R3] Bands across all parts: HD+ 100% means no errors at all; HD 85–99% requires safety correctly and comprehensively addressed, deep understanding, no incomplete or incorrect answers, and a valid reference where requested; Distinction (75–84%) misses some detail; Credit (65–74%) shows moderate understanding, safety not fully addressed; Pass (50–64%) permits some incomplete or wrong answers with fairly well-addressed safety and a valid reference when requested; Marginal Fail (35–49%) follows from unsafe recommendations, non-addressing, or incoherence; Fail (1–34%) adds major omissions such as references; and 0% covers non-submission, use of AI, any integrity breach, or no references when requested. [R3] Because late submissions must arrive first, allow up to twelve days for marks and feedback, released by Monday 14 December. [R3]

## 5. Academic integrity and declarations

SCU defines academic integrity through honesty, fairness, trustworthiness, courage, responsibility, and respect, operating under the Academic Integrity Framework. [R1][R4][R3] Breach categories listed across the briefs: unacceptable GenAI use, unacknowledged or over-limit GenAI, poor referencing, misidentified quotations, close paraphrasing, plagiarism, recycling, misrepresentation, collusion, cheating, contract cheating, and fabricating information. [R1][R4][R3] GenAI is acceptable unless beyond the limit defined for the assessment item. [R1][R4][R3]

Levels differ by task. The quiz is Level 3 — no GenAI anywhere. [R1] The workbook and case study are Level 2: Grammarly for language only in both, and in the case study additionally general search engines (even AI-assisted) for initial information gathering, provided AI outputs never become the answer or the citation. [R2][R4][R3][R5] In every task you may be asked to demonstrate, explain, or provide evidence of your GenAI use or non-use; the Evidence of GenAI Use Guide gives evidence types and a template, and unacknowledged use may breach the Student Academic and Non-Academic Misconduct Rules, Section 3. [R1][R4][R3]

Declaration mechanics: the quiz demands agreement to its conditions on entry; the workbook requires a signed affirmation with name and student number stating your AI use, worth 0/30 without it; the case study affirmation is identical, worth 0/50 without it. [R1][R2][R5] The workbook adds that plagiarism software detects rewritten collaborative work. [R4]

## Common confusions

**"Word limit" vs "suggested words".** The workbook has a real limit — 600 words per question with 10% tolerance, enforced through the rubric. [R4][R2] The case-study counts are explicitly suggestions, with penalties only above twice the suggested number. [R3][R5] Applying either rule to the other task misreads both briefs.

**Where the safety detail goes.** In the workbook, "most important side effects" means the most serious plus the most commonly experienced — two lists. [R2] In the case study, the carbimazole question separates extremely rare effects (Stevens-Johnson syndrome, 2 cases; pancreatitis, 0.03%) from the two rare-but-more-frequent serious effects you are meant to discuss. [R5] Spending scarce words on the ultra-rare list is the warned-against trap.

**Referencing runs one direction only.** The quiz needs none. [R1] Workbook tutorial content needs none, but anything beyond the tutorial must be cited APA 7th, and its absence is part of the Fail description. [R2][R4] The case study inverts: a valid reference is demanded where requested, citations must appear in the cited sources, and citing a module or workshop defeats the purpose. [R3]

**24-hour window is not the duration.** Access opens 06:00 Friday and closes 05:59 Saturday, but once inside you have exactly 60 minutes, one sitting, no backtracking. [R1]

**Sharing answers fails twice.** Question order, option order, and the drawn questions differ per student, so traded answers may be wrong for the recipient — and plagiarism software detects rewritten collaborative work. [R1][R4]

**One orientation page says "final exam".** The notes-folder advice appears both with exam wording and quiz wording; the T5 2026 structure is exactly the three briefs' tasks, and the strategy applies to the open-notes Week 2 quiz: prepare navigable notes because live look-ups are too slow. [S7][S11][V2]
