Module overview: The endocrine system
The endocrine system is essentially a communication system. It depends on feedback loops of the hormones released by internal glands directly into the circulatory system that regulate distant target organs. But what happens when that communication system breaks down? This module answers that question.
This entire module contains a total of 70 minutes of video lessons plus activities that reinforce your knowledge and prepare you for the quiz.

ADH water saver (not compulsory for this unit)
-
-
Vasopressin - The  antidiuretic hormone
The material in this lesson will not be assessed in PHAR2001.
Human vasopressin, also called antidiuretic hormone (ADH) is a hormone synthesized from the hypothalamus. It then travels down the axon terminating in the posterior pituitary, and is released into the circulation in response to hyperosmolality (equivalent to dehydration).
ADH is the water saver, and (its first metabolite) saves the water at its last chance of being reabsorbed at the level of the kidneys just before it is excreted into the urinary bladder. As you would expect, as ADH saves water, it has the effect of increasing the blood pressure.
Diabetes insipidus    is a disorder characterised by a lack of ADH and while it is relatively uncommon, it is important to understand ADH to understand desmopressin (the treatment for diabetes insipidus) and to understand many drugs, because there are several that affect ADH. For instance, caffeine decreases ADH release and ethanol (drinking alcohol) decreases its production, both leading to their 'diuretic' effects. So let's find out more about this important hormone in this lesson.

Understanding thyroid hormone

Introduction to the thyroid hormone
If you could design a hormone that could be released when you are cold, what would it do? It would speed up the metabolism of nearly all cells in the body. The sodium-potassium ATPase pumps all through the body would start working overtime, the body would get much more sensitive to adrenaline.
Well, that is the thyroid hormone. It is also important in growth, metabolism and is absolutely necessary for human life. Let's learn more about the thyroid hormone by engaging in this lesson.

Overview Stress Axis
-
-
Overview of the corticosteroids
In this lesson, we will start understanding the stress access. When we are dying of dehydration or of hypoglycaemia, there is a cascade of hormones that ultimately results in the adrenal cortex secreting the stress hormones. Some of those are termed corticosteroids, and others include noradrenaline. The Corticosteroids include the GLUCOcorticoids which save glucose and the MINERALcorticoids that save salt. These hormones are so valuable in times of stress that they are the obvious choice to mimic in a pharmacological sense.
Please take time to understand this overview of the corticosteroids before getting into the lessons for this chapter.
