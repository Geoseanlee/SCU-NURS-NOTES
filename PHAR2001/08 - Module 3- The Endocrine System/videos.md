## PHAR2001_3_1_5_Growth_Hormone
- Link: https://scu.au.panopto.com/Panopto/Pages/Viewer.aspx?id=469f38c1-5d65-43bf-be9a-b47c01054d71
- Source: Panopto
- Duration: 7:41

**AI summary**

The lecture delved into the anterior pituitary gland and its primary hormone, growth hormone. Growth hormone is an anabolic hormone that promotes the growth of muscles, bones, and cells, and also plays a role in gluconeogenesis. Excesses of growth hormone can lead to increased blood glucose levels and potential hypoglycemia. Growth hormone deficiency, which can occur during childhood or adulthood, can have profound effects on growth and development. Childhood growth hormone deficiency is often idiopathic, but can also be caused by factors like lack of sleep or disrupted sleep patterns. In adults, growth hormone deficiency is usually due to a pituitary tumor and can result in decreased quality of life and energy. The treatment for growth hormone deficiency is somatotropin. Conversely, growth hormone excess can lead to gigantism in children and acromegaly in adults, both of which are associated with significant increases in morbidity and decreased life expectancy. The treatment for growth hormone excess typically involves a synthetic analogue of somatostatin, as well as surgical excision of the pituitary tumor.
