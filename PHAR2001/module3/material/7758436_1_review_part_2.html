The two hormones secreted from the posterior pituitary gland are oxytocin, 'the love hormone' and ADH, 'the water saver'. Those hormones are simply stored in the posterior pituitary, because the hypothalamus synthesises them and controls their release.

The hypothalamus secretes its hormones into the hypothalamus pituitary portal venous system, which allows small amounts of hormones to directly affect the pituitary gland.

Growth hormone, Thyroid stimulating hormone, ACTH, Follicle stimulating hormone Luteinizing hormone, and prolactin are all secreted from the anterior pituitary gland.

The posterior pituitary gland is a neural extension of the hypothalamus and secretes AntiDiuretic Hormone (ADH) and Oxytocin.

The hypothalamus is a major organ in the maintenance of homeostasis, as it receives information in the form of cytokines (messengers of the immune system), hormones, neural messages and other forms of communication, responding by its own neural and hormonal messages.

Stress, pain, body weight, temperature, emotions, various hormones and other stimuli are able to be perceived and responded to by the pituitary gland.
