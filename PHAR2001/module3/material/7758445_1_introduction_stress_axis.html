Objectives

The objectives for this chapter include:

  • Name and explain the actions of the two types of steroidal hormones released from the adrenal cortex (glucocorticoids and mineralocorticoids – the androgens are not as important so concentrate on the first two). 
  • Using hypoglycaemia as the stressor explain why the ‘hypothalamus > anterior pituitary > adrenal cortex’ axis is considered the stress axis (in other words, how does this axis help out in hypoglycaemia?)
  • Using dehydration or hypovolemic shock as the stressor explain why the ‘hypothalamus > anterior pituitary > adrenal cortex’ axis is considered the stress axis (How does the adrenal gland help in times of dehydration?) Drugs to know: Spironolactone and Fludrocortisone
  • Detail the physiological actions of the glucocorticoids. Give specific examples of the application of glucocorticoids in pharmacology, using specific drugs as examples (Methylprednisolone, Prednisolone and Dexamethasone). List ten specific adverse effects of glucocorticoids.

## ppt/slides/slide12.xml
Effects of glucocorticoids 
(Mainly cortisol; a steroidal hormone)
Cortisol is essential in times of trauma and stress:
Affects glucose metabolism
Stimulates gluconeogenesis
Increases blood glucose
Stimulating tissues to use free fatty acids for energy instead of glucose
Stimulates protein breakdown
Inhibits protein syntheses in all body cells
Stimulates hunger
Promotes fat buildup in the trunk and facial regions
Increases the effects of catecholamines - increased heart rate / blood pressure
Inhibition of the immunity and inflammatory 
Strong effect on emotional stability and mood
.

## ppt/slides/slide16.xml
Clinical manifestations of Cushing’s 
Altered fat metabolism leading to fat pads on the back, moon face, protruding abdomen with thin extremities, stretch marks on breasts, thighs, and abdominal surface.
Muscle weakness from protein breakdown
Hypertension as a result of increased catecholamine responsiveness
Weight gain resulting from strong appetite stimulation.  Because of effects on hepatic gluconeogenesis, a reversible form of diabetes mellitus may result.
Inhibition of immune and inflammatory reactions
, leading to poor wound healing

## ppt/slides/slide18.xml
Adrenal dysfunction after long term corticosteroid treatment
Secondary adrenal insufficiency - hypopituitarism / hypothalamic dysfunction
ACTH is not released
Lack of mostly glucocorticoids, but possibly also androgens and mineralocorticoids  
This can occur if cortisol treatment is withdrawn suddenly

Clinical manifestations of 
secondary adrenal insufficiency
Following sudden withdrawal from corticosteroid treatment, the client may experience:
Depression 
(because cortisol levels influence mood and emotions)
Fatigue,

Anorexia, vomiting, diarrhea, and nausea
Inability to respond to stressful situations
(perhaps leading to severe hypotension and shock)
