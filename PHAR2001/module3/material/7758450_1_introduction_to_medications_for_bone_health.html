Objectives

The objectives for this chapter include:

  • Explain the effects of parathyroid hormone and calcitonin and their role in bone health.
  • Explain the process of bone formation and reformation
  • Using two specific examples (bisphosphonates and teriparatide), explain how pharmaceutical agents can be beneficial for people with bone loss. Explain the main adverse drug reactions of these medications.

     PTH: The hormone of the parathyroid gland

       Parathyroid Hormone (PTH)
        Increased release in response to decreased
         serum calcium levels
        Increases osteoclastic activity
        Liberates free calcium
        Decreases excretion of calcium by the kidneys
       Calcitonin is released from the thyroid gland
        Responds to high serum calcium
        Inhibits osteoclastic activity and formation

      Important bone disorders

     Osteoporosis
        • Decreased bone mass and density
     Osteomalacia and Rickets
        • Softening of the bones due to decreased mineralisation
        • Most commonly caused by inadequate Vitamin D
     Osteonecrosis
        • Cellular death of bone cells due to inadequate blood
          supply
     Paget’s disease
        • Abnormal enlargement and weakening of the bone

                Glucocorticoid induced bone disease

              Leading cause of secondary & iatrogenic osteoporosis.
              Lumbar spine & proximal femur.
              2 phases:
                6-12% loss in first year.

                3% loss pa thereafter.

                Rapid increase in risk of hip and vertebral fracture.

                Fracture risk increases by 75% in first 3 months of
                 treatment before a decline in BMD.
              Osteonecrosis may be as high as 40% of patients
                Osteonecrosis is death of bone due to decreased blood
                 supply              NEJM 2011; 365:62-70

      Prevention of osteoporosis
     Calcium and vitamin D supplements.
       • Reduce rate of bone loss.
       • Vitamin D 1000iu/day.

     No calcium supplements after about 50 y/o due to
     increased risk of MI
     Exercise, especially weight bearing exercise before
     the age of 30

         Treatment of osteoporosis
     First Line:
        • Oral Bisphosphonates
            • Inhibit bone breakdown and resorption
            • Selective localisation and prolonged storage in
              bone
            • Increase BMI in the femoral neck and lumbar
              spine.

          • Osteonecrosis of the jaw.

           Oral bisphosphonates
                 (The first ‘s’ is normally silent)

     Oral bisphosphonates end in …dronate:

     •    Alendronate (Fosamax)
     •    Risedronate (Actonel)
     •    Pamidronate (Aredia)
     •    Zoledronate (Zometa, Aclasta)

                 Once yearly infusion

        …oxifene medications like raloxifene, tamoxifen,

        Agonist at the bone (acts like oestrogen at the

        Other treatments for osteoporosis
         Teriparatide, the only anabolic treatment
           Recombinant human parathyroid hormone; identical to
           a portion of human PTH
           Intermittent use activates osteoblasts more than
           osteoclasts
           It is effective in growing bone (8% increase in bone
           density in the spine after one year)

           Daily s/c injection
