## ppt/slides/slide5.xml
Definitions
The 
endocrine system:
 is a system of glands that    secrete hormones directly into the circulatory system. From there, the hormones are often carried to a distant organ. 
The endocrine system is in contrast to the exocrine system, which secretes its hormones using ducts. 
Hormones
: 
From the Greek word meaning "impetus“; A biochemical that is produced by glands in the body transported ** by the circulatory system to another organ to regulate physiological and behavioural activities. They serve as a form of communication
**A few hormones act locally as well (oestrogen, testosterone).

## ppt/slides/slide6.xml
Hormones 
Chemical messengers normally secreted into the bloodstream
Specific rates and rhythms of secretion
Diurnal, pulsatile and cyclic, and patterns depending on circulating substances
Operate within feedback systems
Affect only cells with appropriate receptors
The liver inactivates hormones, rendering the hormones more water soluble for renal excretion

Stimulate protein synthesis 
Activate or deactivate enzyme systems

Regulation of 
hormone release
Hormones are released:
In response to an alteration in the cellular environment
To maintain a regulated level of certain substances or other hormones
Hormones are regulated by chemical, hormonal, or neural factors
Negative feedback
Positive feedback (rarely)

## ppt/slides/slide18.xml
ADH (vasopressin)
Too little ADH
, the water saver results in a condition termed 
diabetes insipidus
. 
“Diabetes” comes Ancient Greek 
δι
αβήτης (diabētēs) which literally means “a passer through; a siphon”
"Insipidus" comes from the Latin: 
in-
 "not" + 
sapidus
 "tasty" from 
sapere
 "have a taste“
Diabetes insipidus is a disease with tasteless urine (in contrast to having sweet urine in diabetes mellitus.) Patients get dehydrated because they cannot save water.

## ppt/slides/slide21.xml
Oxytocin 
Derived from the Greek words meaning ‘swift birth’
 The "bonding hormone“, the “love hormone”
Used to initiate 
labour
, control postpartum 
haemorrhage
, (promote breast milk production - no longer in humans)
Relatively safe when used at recommended doses, and side effects are uncommon
Has a half-life of about three minutes when given intravenously. Intranasal oxytocin has a duration of at least 2.25 hours
Replaces the ergot alkaloid 
ergometrine
 as the first choice in the uses above, but combinations are still used

## ppt/slides/slide23.xml
Growth hormone
Increases muscle mass through sarcomere hypertrophy
Promotes lipolysis
Increases protein synthesis
Your client needs to be told how dangerous it is to use growth hormone for muscle growth!
 Stimulates the growth of all internal organs excluding the brain
Promotes gluconeogenesis in the liver (increases glucose levels: diabetes!)

## ppt/slides/slide24.xml
Growth hormone deficiency
Growth hormone deficiency (GDH) in childhood is commonly idiopathic (no identifiable cause)
Adult-onset GHD is commonly due to pituitary tumours 
Somatropin
 is given for growth hormone deficiencies
Subcutaneous or intramuscular injections

## ppt/slides/slide25.xml
Growth hormone excess
Gigantism occurs when the child is still growing. 
Acromegaly occurs after the epiphyseal plates have closed. 
Lanreotide
 is a synthetic analogue of somatostatin, a naturally occurring inhibitory hormone which blocks the release of growth hormone.
