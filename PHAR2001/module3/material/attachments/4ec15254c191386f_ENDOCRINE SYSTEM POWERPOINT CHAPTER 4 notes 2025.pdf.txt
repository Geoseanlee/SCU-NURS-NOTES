                        How the stress axis works
               CRH released from the hypothalamus in response to:
                 Stress
                 Hypoglycemia
                 Decreased circulating levels of glucocorticoids

               Adrenocorticotropin hormone (ACTH) is released from
                the pituitary when stimulated by CRH, releasing
                   Glucocorticoids,
                   Mineralocorticoids (salt savers)
                   Androgens
               …From the adrenal cortex

                              Effects of glucocorticoids
                               (Mainly cortisol; a steroidal hormone)

             Cortisol is essential in times of trauma and stress:
               Affects glucose metabolism
               Stimulates gluconeogenesis
               Increases blood glucose
               Stimulating tissues to use free fatty acids for energy
                instead of glucose
               Stimulates protein breakdown
               Inhibits protein syntheses in all body cells
               Stimulates hunger
               Promotes fat buildup in the trunk and facial regions
               Increases the effects of catecholamines - increased heart
                rate / blood pressure
               Inhibition of the immunity and inflammatory
               Strong effect on emotional stability and mood .

               Clinical manifestations of Cushing’s

  Altered fat metabolism leading to fat pads on
   the back, moon face, protruding abdomen with
   thin extremities, stretch marks on breasts,
   thighs, and abdominal surface.
  Muscle weakness from protein breakdown
  Hypertension as a result of increased
   catecholamine responsiveness
  Weight gain resulting from strong appetite
   stimulation. Because of effects on hepatic
   gluconeogenesis, a reversible form of diabetes
   mellitus may result.
  Inhibition of immune and inflammatory reactions,
   leading to poor wound healing

     Adrenal dysfunction after long
     term corticosteroid treatment

     • Secondary adrenal insufficiency -
       hypopituitarism / hypothalamic dysfunction
     • ACTH is not released
     • Lack of mostly glucocorticoids, but possibly
       also androgens and mineralocorticoids

      This can occur if cortisol treatment is withdrawn
                          suddenly

        Clinical manifestations of
     secondary adrenal insufficiency

     Following sudden withdrawal from corticosteroid
     treatment, the client may experience:
     • Depression
       (because cortisol levels influence mood and emotions)

     • Fatigue,
       (related to hypoglycemia, and decreased gluconeogenesis)

     • Anorexia, vomiting, diarrhea, and nausea
     • Inability to respond to stressful situations
       (perhaps leading to severe hypotension and shock)
