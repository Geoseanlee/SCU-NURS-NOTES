Vasopressin - The antidiuretic hormone

The material in this lesson will not be assessed in PHAR2001.  

Human vasopressin, also called antidiuretic hormone (ADH) is a hormone synthesized from the hypothalamus. It then travels down the axon terminating in the posterior pituitary, and is released into the circulation in response to hyperosmolality (equivalent to dehydration).

ADH is the water saver, and (its first metabolite) saves the water at its last chance of being reabsorbed at the level of the kidneys just before it is excreted into the urinary bladder. As you would expect, as ADH saves water, it has the effect of increasing the blood pressure. 

Diabetes insipidus is a disorder characterised by a lack of ADH and while it is relatively uncommon, it is important to understand ADH to understand desmopressin (the treatment for diabetes insipidus) and to understand many drugs, because there are several that affect ADH. For instance, caffeine decreases ADH release and ethanol (drinking alcohol) decreases its production, both leading to their 'diuretic' effects. So let's find out more about this important hormone in this lesson.

ADH is antidiuretic hormone, the water saver.

When you awaken from a night where you drank too much ethanol (the alchol in beverages) the decreased circulating ADH contributes to the hangover, and is the reason your tongue is stuck to the roof of your mouth.

Some drugs affect ADH secretion or actions, and as such may result in inadvertent diuretic or antidiuretic effects.

Desmopressin is the treatment of ADH insufficiency.

ADH is secreted from the anterior pituitary gland

Desmopressin is a synthetic form of ADH that is used to treat diabetes insipidus. it is a synthetic form of the normal human hormone arginine vasopressin (the antidiuretic hormone; ADH)

Desmopressin is commonly taken intranasally, and as such, the client should be informed of the proper way to administer the drug.

Desmopressin is also prescribed for bed wetting, nocturea (nighttime urination). When the person who does not have diabetes insipidus takes desmopressin, it has a risk of causing hyponatremia (low sodium in the blood) and siezures, especially if they have perspired through the day.

A client taking desmopressin for bed wetting or nocturea who does not have diabetes insipidus should consume very little salt and plenty of water through the day.
