Introduction to the thyroid hormone

If you could design a hormone that could be released when you are cold, what would it do? It would speed up the metabolism of nearly all cells in the body. The sodium-potassium ATPase pumps all through the body would start working overtime, the body would get much more sensitive to adrenaline.

Well, that is the thyroid hormone. It is also important in growth, metabolism and is absolutely necessary for human life. Let's learn more about the thyroid hormone by engaging in this lesson.

The thyroid axis begins at the hypothalamus secretion of Thyroid releasing hormone (TRH), which incites the pituitary to release Thyroid stimulating hormone (TSH), which incites the thyroid gland to release free T4.

The T4 (4 molecules of iodine) released from the thyroid gland is LESS potent than the T3 (3 molecules of iodine) that has been metabolised in the liver

The main stimulus for the release of TRH from the hypothalamus is exposure to cold. In turn, thyroid hormone increases the metabolism in nearly every cell in the body, and increases the sodium-potassium ATPase pumps throughout the body, increasing the body’s heat.

One of the actions of the thyroid hormone is to increase the effects of adrenaline and noradrenaline (the catecholamines)

The thyroid gland is made to sequester the iodine from the bloodstream, and if it is not receiving adequate supply, a type of goitre or enlargement of the gland will occur.

Since the thyroid hormone uses iodine in its structure, supplementation of iodine will increase the amount of thyroid hormone produced.
