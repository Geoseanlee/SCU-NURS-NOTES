Chapter objectives

The objectives for this chapter include:

  • Define the ‘endocrine system’ and ‘hormone’
  • Give an example of a homeostatic control mechanism involving ‘negative feedback’.
  • Explain the reasons that the hypothalamus is thought to be the controlling organ of the endocrine system.
  • Explain the effects of the two hormones secreted from the posterior pituitary gland (oxytocin and ADH) and give an example of when they would be useful as a pharmaceutical agent.
  • Explain the actions of growth hormones and the results of excesses and deficiencies during childhood and after puberty. 
  • Drugs to know: Desmopressin, somatropin, vasopressin, oxytocin

                    Definitions
    The endocrine system: is a system of glands that
    secrete hormones directly into the circulatory system.
    From there, the hormones are often carried to a distant
    organ.
       • The endocrine system is in contrast to the exocrine system,
         which secretes its hormones using ducts.
    Hormones: From the Greek word meaning "impetus“; A
    biochemical that is produced by glands in the body
    transported ** by the circulatory system to another
    organ to regulate physiological and behavioural
    activities. They serve as a form of communication
          **A few hormones act locally as well (oestrogen, testosterone).



5




                        Hormones
     • Chemical messengers normally secreted into the
       bloodstream
     • Specific rates and rhythms of secretion
       • Diurnal, pulsatile and cyclic, and patterns
          depending on circulating substances

     • Operate within feedback systems
     • Affect only cells with appropriate receptors

     The liver inactivates hormones, rendering the hormones
               more water soluble for renal excretion

17




                              ADH (vasopressin)
      Too little ADH, the water saver results in a
       condition termed diabetes insipidus.
      “Diabetes” comes Ancient Greek διαβήτης
       (diabētēs) which literally means “a passer
       through; a siphon”
      "Insipidus" comes from the Latin: in- "not" +
       sapidus "tasty" from sapere "have a taste“
      Diabetes insipidus is a disease with
       tasteless urine (in contrast to having sweet
       urine in diabetes mellitus.) Patients get
       dehydrated because they cannot save water.

                 ADH (vasopressin) replacement:
                         Desmopressin
      Synthetic version of ADH, the water saver (vasopressin)
      Works like ADH
      Main uses include diabetes insipidus but also used in
        Bed wetting

        Nocturia

      People using the medication for nocturia should be educated
      about hyponatremia
      Often, intranasal administration

20




                                  Oxytocin
         Derived from the Greek words meaning ‘swift birth’
         The "bonding hormone“, the “love hormone”
         Used to initiate labour, control postpartum haemorrhage,
          (promote breast milk production - no longer in humans)
         Relatively safe when used at recommended doses, and side
          effects are uncommon
         Has a half-life of about three minutes when given
          intravenously. Intranasal oxytocin has a duration of at least
          2.25 hours
         Replaces the ergot alkaloid ergometrine as the first choice in
          the uses above, but combinations are still used

              Growth hormone

     • Increases muscle mass through sarcomere hypertrophy
     • Promotes lipolysis
     • Increases protein synthesis
     • Your client needs to be told how dangerous it is to use growth
       hormone for muscle growth!
        • Stimulates the growth of all internal organs excluding the
          brain
        • Promotes gluconeogenesis in the liver (increases glucose
          levels: diabetes!)




23




     Growth hormone deficiency

      Growth hormone deficiency (GDH) in childhood
       is commonly idiopathic (no identifiable cause)
      Adult-onset GHD is commonly due to pituitary
       tumours

      Somatropin is given for growth hormone
       deficiencies
      Subcutaneous or intramuscular injections
