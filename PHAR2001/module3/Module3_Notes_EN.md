## Feedback before drug names

Map the gland, hormone, target tissue and feedback signal. Distinguish insufficient hormone production from excessive production and from impaired tissue response. This explains why replacement and suppression have different goals.

## Pituitary-related hormones

The chapter introduces oxytocin and reviews ADH/vasopressin and growth hormone. The source marks the ADH and growth-hormone lessons as non-compulsory; they remain archived. Do not assume every archived lesson has the same assessment status.

## The thyroid axis

Connect thyroid hormone with metabolic activity and contrast hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism. The hyperthyroidism lesson is marked non-compulsory. Use the axis to explain what replacement or reduction of thyroid activity is trying to achieve.

## Diabetes coverage

The chapter explains that diabetes is developed through tutorials and case studies rather than a normal standalone video chapter. Review the archived tutorial materials as well as the module outline; an empty chapter folder does not prove the subject is absent from the unit.

## Adrenal corticosteroids

Relate stress-axis regulation and glucocorticoid actions to therapeutic effects and the risks of prolonged exposure. Understand why physiological feedback and treatment duration matter. Any changes to steroid treatment require the applicable clinical guidance, not a study-note inference.

## Bone health

Link bone breakdown and formation with osteoporosis and endocrine control. The chapter covers bisphosphonates, teriparatide and SERMs. Explain which part of bone regulation each approach is intended to affect rather than treating them as interchangeable supplements.

## Endocrine comparisons

| Topic | Mechanism to explain | Important distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Pituitary-related hormones | Oxytocin, ADH/vasopressin and growth hormone connect hormone action to a target tissue | The source marks ADH and growth-hormone lessons as optional; retain them without relabelling them as required |
| Thyroid | The axis links hormone production, feedback and metabolic effects; hypothyroid treatment replaces deficient hormone | Hyperthyroid treatment has a different aim; the hyperthyroid extension is optional |
| Glucocorticoids | Stress-axis feedback and broad metabolic/immune effects explain benefits and adverse effects | Prolonged exposure can suppress physiological hormone production; treatment changes require clinical guidance |
| Mineralocorticoid-related drugs | Salt/water regulation links adrenal physiology with fluid balance; compare fludrocortisone and spironolactone in the source | Do not confuse glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid actions simply because both are adrenal steroids |
| Bone medicines | Bone remodelling balances resorption and formation; the chapter compares bisphosphonates, teriparatide and SERMs | These target different parts of the process and are not interchangeable calcium supplements |

## Revision task

Draw the hypothalamus–pituitary–target-gland feedback loop and mark where deficiency, excess and a medicine alter it. For corticosteroids, connect each proposed benefit with a possible consequence of prolonged exposure rather than memorising an unrelated adverse-effect list. Locate the tutorial/case-study diabetes material separately: its placement outside a normal video chapter is deliberate.
