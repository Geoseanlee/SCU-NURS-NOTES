C/3: Antifungal agents
-
-
A brief look at antifungal agents
Fungi are nature's way of degrading things, so if you want your belongings to last longer, make sure that you clean off the mould and mildew!
Fungi are found all over, and we have an astounding number of fungi that live in the natural skin flora, but the bacterial toxins from the resident bacteria keep the fungi in check. When an infection does occur from fungi, the term is a mycosis. That is where our antifungal infections come in, and unfortunately, because of the fact that fungal cells are so close to human cells, there is potential toxicity with most antifungal agents. The main difference between human cells and the fungal cells is that human cells contain cholesterol in the cell membrane, while fungal cell membranes contain ergosterol. So the ergosterol is the target of most of our antifungal agents.

Antiviral agents
We knew very little about viruses until the HIV scare in the early 1980s but since then our understanding of viruses has been advancing comparatively quickly. ===== FILE: 09 - B-2- Specific antibiotics.epub =====
B/2: Specific antibiotics
-
-
Specific Antibiotics 1
Our antibiotics can be grouped in many different ways. For instance, one grouping has them as time dependant versus dose dependent. But one of the most useful ways to group them is by mechanism of action. While we will not need to know the mechanism by which various types of antibiotics work, it is a convenient grouping that ultimately makes it easier to get a complete picture of each type of antibiotic. In this first lesson about specific antibiotics, we will look at the antibiotics that inhibit cell wall synthesis. This happens as the bacteria are reproducing, and the prodigy will be unable to make the cell wall needed to survive. Antibiotics that inhibit the cell wall synthesis include the penicillins (suffix: cillin) and the cephalosporins (prefix: cef or ceph). These contain a β (beta)-lactam ring that is the active portion of the drug. Bacteria often develop resistance to β-lactam antibiotics by synthesizing a β-lactamase, an enzyme that attacks the β-lactam ring before it can adversely affect the cell wall. To overcome this resistance, β-lactam antibiotics can be given with β-lactamase inhibitors such as clavulanic acid.

===== FILE: 10 - Specific Antibiotics 2.epub =====
Specific Antibiotics 2
-
-
Specific antibiotics continued
In this lesson, we broaden our understanding of ways in which medications can kill bacteria with antibiotics that inhibit DNA, block protein synthesis and inhibit metabolic processes.
For instance, Sulphonamides including sulphamethoxazole inhibits the ability of the bacteria to synthesise its folic acid that is needed to reproduce. Those medications are bacteriostatic, keeping the microorganisms in check while the immune system gains control.
