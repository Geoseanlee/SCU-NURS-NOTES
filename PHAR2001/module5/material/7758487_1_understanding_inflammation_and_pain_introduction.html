The physiology and pathology of pain

Pain is a protective mechanism. It tells us when we need to tend to injuries, and helps further complications develop on those injuries. But when pain becomes chronic, there are permanent changes that magnify and perpetuate the pain. This has led to chronic pain now being considered a disorder in its own right. Given the importance of pain and the percentage of individuals living in chronic pain (20% of Australians), it is important that we learn as much about pain as we can.

## ppt/slides/slide39.xml
Fever
A normal adaptive response to endogenous or exogenous 
pyrogens
 
A trigger of the fever, called a pyrogen, crosses the BBB and causes a release of prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) at the hypothalamus. PGE2 then in turn acts on the hypothalamus, which generates a systemic response back to the rest of the body, causing heat-creating effects to match a new temperature level.
 
Exogenous pyrogens 
lipopolysaccharide complex in the cell wall of gram-positive bacteria (mediated by secondary inflammatory molecules)
Endogenous pyrogens 
Inflammatory cytokines (Interleukin 1 and 6, tumour necrosis factor and interferon gamma) break into the BBB, signalling the release of prostaglandin E2 which acts on the hypothalamus

                                    Fever
     • A normal adaptive response to endogenous or exogenous pyrogens
     • A trigger of the fever, called a pyrogen, crosses the BBB and causes a
       release of prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) at the hypothalamus. PGE2 then in
       turn acts on the hypothalamus, which generates a systemic response
       back to the rest of the body, causing heat-creating effects to match a
       new temperature level.
     • Exogenous pyrogens
         • lipopolysaccharide complex in the cell wall of gram-positive
           bacteria (mediated by secondary inflammatory molecules)
     • Endogenous pyrogens
         • Inflammatory cytokines (Interleukin 1 and 6, tumour necrosis factor
           and interferon gamma) break into the BBB, signalling the release of
           prostaglandin E2 which acts on the hypothalamus

                                  Antipyretics
            Before giving antipyretics, please be aware of the benefits of fever:
     • Increased temperature kills many micro-organisms
     • Increased temperature has adverse effects on the growth and replication
       of others
     • Higher body temperatures decrease serum levels of iron, zinc and copper
       needed for bacterial replication
     • Increased temperature prevents viral replication in infected cells
     • Fever is one of the inflammatory factors that promotes liver to produce
       acute-phase proteins
     • Heat binds cations necessary for bacterial reproduction
     • Heat increases lymphocytic transformation
     • Heat increases motility of polymorphonuclear neutrophils
     • Phagocytosis is enhanced during fever
     • Production of antiviral interferon may be augmented during fever
