                       What is pain?
    • Pain is an emotion and a sensation.
       • Protective mechanism
       • Detects, localises and identifies tissue damaging
         processors.
    • Pain varies between individuals.
       • Subjective experience that varies in individuals
          • Physical
          • Psychological (anxiety, memory)
          • Environmental factors
    • Pain varies between individuals and situation e.g.
      during a battle or childbirth.
    • Pain is affected by mental state, anxiety, expectation of
      pain, past experiences and many other factors

                        Types of pain
    • Acute Pain:
       • Tissue damage
       • Inflammatory cascade
    • Sub-acute phase:
       • Transition from acute to chronic pain
       • 4 month window (aggressive treatment indicated now)
       • Neuroplastic changes to CNS
    • Chronic pain:
       • CNS becomes sensitized
       • Responds excessively to stimuli
          • Debilitating, expensive & effects everyone surrounding
            patient
          • Chronic pain begins to have similarities with neuropathic pain

           Acute verses chronic pain
     • Chronic pain decreases level of endogenous
       opioids in the spinal cord
     • Changes in dorsal root ganglion include
       inflammation that increases transmission of
       impulses.
     • Chronic pain often results in loss of appetite,
       sleep disturbances and depression
     • Substance P is a neurotransmitter that lasts
       MUCH longer than ideal

                                    Fever
     • A normal adaptive response to endogenous or exogenous pyrogens
     • A trigger of the fever, called a pyrogen, crosses the BBB and causes a
       release of prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) at the hypothalamus. PGE2 then in
       turn acts on the hypothalamus, which generates a systemic response
       back to the rest of the body, causing heat-creating effects to match a
       new temperature level.
     • Exogenous pyrogens
         • lipopolysaccharide complex in the cell wall of gram-positive
           bacteria (mediated by secondary inflammatory molecules)
     • Endogenous pyrogens
         • Inflammatory cytokines (Interleukin 1 and 6, tumour necrosis factor
           and interferon gamma) break into the BBB, signalling the release of
           prostaglandin E2 which acts on the hypothalamus

                                  Antipyretics
            Before giving antipyretics, please be aware of the benefits of fever:
     • Increased temperature kills many micro-organisms
     • Increased temperature has adverse effects on the growth and replication
       of others
     • Higher body temperatures decrease serum levels of iron, zinc and copper
       needed for bacterial replication
     • Increased temperature prevents viral replication in infected cells
     • Fever is one of the inflammatory factors that promotes liver to produce
       acute-phase proteins
     • Heat binds cations necessary for bacterial reproduction
     • Heat increases lymphocytic transformation
     • Heat increases motility of polymorphonuclear neutrophils
     • Phagocytosis is enhanced during fever
     • Production of antiviral interferon may be augmented during fever

                    Paracetamol toxicity
      Paracetamol toxicity is the number one cause of liver failure
                  in Australia, UK, USA, NZ and Canada.
     • Toxicity - At risk if:
        • Doses > 4g / day
        • Person metabolizes less quickly (variable half life of 1-4
          hours)
        • Client is after the opioid high: Taking combinations of
          opioids and paracetamol

        Paracetamol is often used for suicide attempts: education
         is necessary! The more you take, the more TOXIC IT IS.
                  High doses do not increase efficacy!

            Tricyclic Antidepressants
      • E.g. *amitriptyline*, nortriptyline
      • Neuropathic pain…smaller doses
      • Drowsiness, dry mouth, constipation, impaired
        cardiac conduction
      • Postural hypotension….elderly. Less with
        nortriptyline
      • Doses above 75mg: adverse effects
      • Response depends on duration of pain
      • Opioid sparring in cancer pain

                  *Carbamazepine*
      • Stabilise membranes/inc effectiveness of
        GABA
      • Start slow and titrate up. 50-100mg od-bd
      • Nausea, dizziness, rash and sedation
      • Take with food
      • Monitor liver, renal and blood counts
      • Enzyme inducer and increases
        metabolism of tramadol and methadone
      • First choice in trigeminal neuralgia

      • Interfere with release NA, substance P &
        glutamate
      • Start low and titrate up
      • Renally excreted
      • Dizziness, somnolence and ataxia
      • Neuropathic pain 900-3600mg/day
      • No drug interactions
      • Short half life
      • PBS
