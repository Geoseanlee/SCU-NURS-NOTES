## ppt/slides/slide39.xml
Fever
A normal adaptive response to endogenous or exogenous 
pyrogens
 
A trigger of the fever, called a pyrogen, crosses the BBB and causes a release of prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) at the hypothalamus. PGE2 then in turn acts on the hypothalamus, which generates a systemic response back to the rest of the body, causing heat-creating effects to match a new temperature level.
 
Exogenous pyrogens 
lipopolysaccharide complex in the cell wall of gram-positive bacteria (mediated by secondary inflammatory molecules)
Endogenous pyrogens 
Inflammatory cytokines (Interleukin 1 and 6, tumour necrosis factor and interferon gamma) break into the BBB, signalling the release of prostaglandin E2 which acts on the hypothalamus 

## ppt/slides/slide40.xml
Antipyretics
Before giving antipyretics, please be aware of the benefits of fever:
Increased temperature kills many micro-organisms
Increased temperature has adverse effects on the growth and replication of others 
Higher body temperatures decrease serum levels of iron, zinc and copper needed for bacterial replication
Increased temperature prevents viral replication in infected cells
Fever is one of the inflammatory factors that promotes liver to produce acute-phase proteins
Heat binds 
cations
 necessary for bacterial reproduction
Heat increases lymphocytic transformation
Heat increases motility of 
polymorphonuclear
 neutrophils 
Phagocytosis is enhanced during fever
Production of antiviral interferon may be augmented during fever

## ppt/slides/slide54.xml
Paracetamol toxicity
Paracetamol toxicity is the number one cause of liver failure in Australia, UK, USA, NZ and Canada.
Toxicity - At risk if: 
Doses > 4g / day
Person metabolizes less quickly (variable half life of 1-4 hours)
Client is after the opioid high: Taking combinations of opioids and paracetamol
Paracetamol is often used for suicide attempts: education is necessary! 
The more you take, the more TOXIC IT IS. High doses do not increase efficacy!
