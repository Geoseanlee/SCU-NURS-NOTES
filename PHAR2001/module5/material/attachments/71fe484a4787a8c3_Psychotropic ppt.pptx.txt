Acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (*donepezil*) 
By blocking the enzyme that degrades acetylcholine, more neurotransmitter will be available and its action prolonged. 
Common adverse effects 
Donepezil produces a number of common adverse effects, which include nausea, vomiting, elevated liver enzyme levels, diarrhoea, dizziness, anorexia, myalgia and dyspepsia
These drugs may reduce deterioration in cognition but do not stop the pathological process or yield a cure

## ppt/slides/slide22.xml
Memantine blocks glutamate at the NMDA receptor
The brain is very vulnerable to glutamate-mediated overexcitation; this results in excitotoxicity. 
Ultimately, with this overexcitation, cell integrity is disrupted and the nerve cell dies. 
Part of the process of Alzheimer's disease may have to do with this excitotoxicity from excess glutamate.  
The psychotropic agent 
memantine is a
 selective NMDA receptor antagonist. 
This decreases calcium influx and decreases excitation

## ppt/slides/slide23.xml
Glutamate receptor antagonists
Memantine is indicated in the management of moderate to severe Alzheimer’s disease. 
Mechanism of action
Memantine is an NMDA receptor antagonist that selectively restores the ungated receptors to the gated state and inhibits prolonged calcium ion influx without affecting normal glutamate neurotransmission.
Common adverse effects
Agitation, insomnia, drowsiness, dizziness, headache, gastrointestinal disturbances and coughing.

Anxiolytics and hypnotics
Anxiolytics, minor tranquiliser and sedative are often used interchangeably and are described as a substance that diminishes the activity of the CNS. Sedatives calm a person receiving therapy, and moderate excitability. 
‘Hypnotic’ is the term used to describe a substance that induces sleep
Often difficult to distinguish
Many of the medicines in these categories can induce psychological dependence

Schizophrenia
Schizophrenia is relatively common, affecting 1 per cent of the population,
Increased dopaminergic activity in certain parts of the brain, particularly the prefrontal cortex, limbic
Onset 15 and 25
Symptoms of schizophrenia are described as either negative or positive. 
Positive symptoms 
delusions, disorganised speech and/or thought, distorted perceptions and emotions, hallucinations and the belief that one is under the control of an external agency. 
Negative symptoms 
toneless speech, vacant eyes, social withdrawal, anhedonia (lack of enjoyment) and apathy.

First generation vs second generation antipsychotics: review
Antipsychotic agents can be classified as either typical (first generation) or atypical agents (second generation). 
Typical are much more active on the D2 receptors than are the atypical ones. 
The atypical agents, have less affinity for the D2 receptors, usually have a lower incidence of debilitating motor disturbances
The goal is to find the right antipsychotic agent for the person: an agent that addresses the most disturbing symptoms without the side effects that may prompt the person to stop taking the medication.

## ppt/slides/slide83.xml
Terms regarding seizures*
*
*The term anticonvulsant is misleading because some seizures do not manifest as convulsions. 
 
Partial seizures – begin in one hemisphere at a single cortical focus 
Generalised seizures form across both hemispheres 
Consciousness is always lost
Epilepsy is a term used to indicate a condition of recurrent seizures
It does not describe the nature of the seizure itself. 
Status epilepticus: the affected person moves immediately from one seizure into another.
Very dangerous unless treated immediately

## ppt/slides/slide90.xml
Carbamazepine 
Carbamazepine (CBZ) is an anti seizure and mood-stabilizing drug used primarily in the treatment of epilepsy and neuropathic pain. 
Generalized seizures
CBZ is a sodium channel blocker . 
Adverse effects may include nausea and drowsiness
Bone marrow dysfunction is possible

## ppt/slides/slide91.xml
Valproic acid 
Valproic acid is an anti seizure and mood-stabilizing drug used primarily in the treatment of epilepsy and bipolar disorder.
Partial and generalized seizures
Valproic acid is a sodium channel blocker (and may increase GABA). 
Side effects include nausea, sleepiness, dry mouth
