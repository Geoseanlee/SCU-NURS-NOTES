Pain management for the elderly and children

In this lesson, we will be learning about the management of pain in children and the elderly. Both of those groups of people have a decreased ability to metabolise and excrete medications. But in addition, there are other specific issue for both groups. For instance, one would normally think that if the dose for a 100 kg adult is 100 mg, the 10 kg child should take 10 mg. But that dose could cause serious harm or death of the child, because dose for children is not calculated that way. For the elderly, they are often on several medications at the same time so that, combined with their inability to metabolise the medications quickly will lead to special issues. Let’s explore the specific issues for these clients in more depth.

Both children and the elderly metabolise analgesics at about the same rate as other humans

The dose of analgesics in children is based on mg of drug per kg in child weight and NEVER should be x% of the adult dose

Aspirin is not to be used in children who have a fever /viral infection because it can (rarely) cause Reye’s syndrome, which is a life threatening

Codeine is contraindicated in children younger than 12 because it is generally ineffective and can cause respiratory depression.
