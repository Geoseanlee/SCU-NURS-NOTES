Current treatment of Alzheimer's disease

Alzheimer’s disease is another chronic, progressive degenerative disorder, but it involves completely different neurons in different portions of the brain. Unlike Parkinson’s disease, there are few changes in movement until late in the course of Alzheimer’s disease. But similar to Parkinson’s disease, we have not found medications to alter the course of Alzheimer’s disease for more than a few months. Let’s take a look at this very important disorder and its treatment.

Donepezil blocks the enzyme that degrades acetylcholine, leading to more acetylcholine in the brain. It delays the deterioration of the brain by about 6 months. Common side effects include GIT symptoms while serious adverse effects include hepatic damage.

Memantine improves neural degeneration in humans

Memantine decreases the glutamate-mediated overexcitation by blocking the NMDA receptors but comes along with agitation, insomnia, drowsiness, dizziness and GIT disturbances.

Memantine is nephrotoxic, and as such should be taken with plenty of water and should never be taken with other nephrotoxic drugs, for instance NSAIDs.
