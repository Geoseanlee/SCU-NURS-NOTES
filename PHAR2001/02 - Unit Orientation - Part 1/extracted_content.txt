Can I use GenAI for assessments?
At Southern Cross University, you are permitted to use Generative AI tools to complete assessments when explicitly told to by your Unit Assessor.
For each assessment, your Unit Assessor will determine GenAI use level as one of the following:
- Level 1: Full GenAI Use - GenAI tools can be used in full throughout the assessment.
- Level 2: Purpose-Specific GenAU Use - GenAI tools can be used for specific assessment tasks or purposes as identified and scaffolded by the Unit Assessor.*
- Level 3: No GenAI Use - GenAI tools must not be used in any part of the assessment.
⚠️ For assessments classified Level 2, the Unit Assessor will specify the permitted and prohibited uses of GenAI within the relevant assessment brief.
Which GenAI tools can I use?
When approved for use in a specific assessment, Southern Cross University recommends that you use Microsoft Copilot with your SCU login. SCU has an enterprise agreement with Microsoft, which means Copilot offers a higher level of data security and privacy than public, free-to-use GenAI tools.
You may also choose to use other widely available tools (e.g. ChatGPT, Google Gemini), but you do so at your own risk. SCU does not control or endorse third-party tools.

How can I use GenAI safely?
Using GenAI can be helpful for your learning, but only if you use it safely and responsibly. Safe use means protecting your privacy, checking accuracy, and making sure GenAI supports (not replaces) your learning.
Your Responsibilities
- Protect your data: Never enter personal details, assessment questions, or sensitive SCU information into GenAI tools. Assume anything you type could be stored or reviewed.
- Check accuracy: GenAI can make mistakes, especially in technical areas like engineering materials. Always verify explanations, formulas, or examples against your lecture notes, labs, or trusted resources.
- Respect assessment rules: Follow the GenAI use level set for each assessment. Using GenAI in prohibited tasks counts as academic misconduct.
- Own your learning: Use GenAI to practise, test, or revise — but the real understanding must come from you.
Reduce Your Risk
- Use Microsoft Copilot with your SCU login where possible. It offers better security under SCU’s enterprise agreement.
- Check tool policies before using external GenAI. Look for how your data is stored, whether it trains the model, and if you can delete it.
- Minimise what you share: Use made-up or anonymised data (e.g. fictional material strengths in practice problems).
- Avoid full uploads: Don’t paste whole assignments or lab reports into GenAI. Work with short sections or sample problems instead.
- Delete history: Turn off chat history/model training where possible and clear conversations when finished.
- Be transparent: Keep a brief note of how you used GenAI, and acknowledge it if required in your assessments.
🔑 Remember: Safe use means GenAI should help you learn without taking away your opportunity to practise, demonstrate, and master core skills.
How can I show I've used GenAI responsibly?
The Unit Assessor may ask you to describe or demonstrate how you used GenAI and complied with the assessment guidelines. Be prepared to discuss which AI tools you used and how you used them.

Academic Integrity at Southern Cross University
Academic integrity is a core expectation for every student at SCU. Whenever you submit work, you are required to follow the Student Academic and Non-Academic Misconduct Rules. These Rules explain the standards for honest scholarship and the consequences if standards are breached. You are responsible for understanding these expectations and applying them in all assessments across your studies.
Not following the Rules can lead to serious penalties, including failing an assessment, failing the unit, or more significant University sanctions. If you ever feel unsure about what academic integrity means in your unit, speak with your Unit Assessor or visit the Academic Integrity website for more guidance.
Start the Academic Integrity Module
If you are starting your SCU studies, you must complete the Academic Integrity Module during your first study period. This module helps you understand academic integrity, the responsible use of sources, referencing, collaboration, and how to avoid common mistakes in academic integrity.
If you do not complete the module:
- Your grades may be withheld
- You may be withdrawn from future enrolled units
- You may be unable to enrol in further study
These penalties remain in effect until the module is successfully completed.
Completing the module usually takes 1 to 2 hours, so it’s best to complete it early in the session.
