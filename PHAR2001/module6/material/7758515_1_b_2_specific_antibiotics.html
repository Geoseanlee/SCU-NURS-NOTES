Specific Antibiotics 1

Our antibiotics can be grouped in many different ways. For instance, one grouping has them as time dependant versus dose dependent. But one of the most useful ways to group them is by mechanism of action. While we will not need to know the mechanism by which various types of antibiotics work, it is a convenient grouping that ultimately makes it easier to get a complete picture of each type of antibiotic. In this first lesson about specific antibiotics, we will look at the antibiotics that inhibit cell wall synthesis. This happens as the bacteria are reproducing, and the prodigy will be unable to make the cell wall needed to survive. Antibiotics that inhibit the cell wall synthesis include the penicillins (suffix: cillin) and the cephalosporins (prefix: cef or ceph). These contain a β (beta)-lactam ring that is the active portion of the drug. Bacteria often develop resistance to β-lactam antibiotics by synthesizing a β-lactamase, an enzyme that attacks the β-lactam ring before it can adversely affect the cell wall. To overcome this resistance, β-lactam antibiotics can be given with β-lactamase inhibitors such as clavulanic acid.
