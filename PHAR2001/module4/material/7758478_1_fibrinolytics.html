Overview of fibrinolysis

The final process involved in haemostasis, or the stoppage of blood, is actually the dissolving of the clot with a proteolytic (protein splitting) enzyme. That process is called fibrinolysis, and can be exploited with our medications, although, as you can imagine, there is a very fine line between the therapeutic dose and the one that can cause harm, or in other words, each of the fibrinolytic drugs have very narrow therapeutic index.

Streptokinase is an oral fibrinolytic (thrombolytic) drug used to prevent deep vein thrombosis (DVTs)

Streptokinase has a very narrow therapeutic index, meaning that there is little difference between the effective dose and the one that is either ineffective or results in haemorrhaging.

Streptokinase is of bacterial origin and as such, may result in allergies or other hypersensitivities.

Worldwide, streptokinase is still the most frequently used fibrinolytic agent.
