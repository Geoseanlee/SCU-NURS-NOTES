Introduction to chapter 2: Hypertension

Nearly 25% of Australians suffer from hypertension, which is a risk factor for numerous chronic disorders. In this chapter, we will explore hypertension, beginning with an understanding of blood pressure regulation, and placing our most important antihypertensive agents into the context of that information. The understanding of blood pressure control is very important in our understanding of antihypertensive drugs because our medications target those physiological mechanisms. 

The objectives for this chapter include:

  • Explain the regulation of blood pressure, including the short-term regulation and the long term regulation.
  • Identify and describe the therapeutic actions and major side effects of common pharmacological agents used in the treatment of hypertension

                 Understanding the Renin Cascade
          • Think of renin as the panic button to simultaneously
            increase blood pressure by every means available

          • When our blood pressure is too low, or there is sympathetic
            nervous system stimulation to the kidneys, renin is
            released.

          • The ultimate effect is an increase of the blood pressure by
            at least 5 mechanisms

          • Our medications of the renin cascade attempt to stop this
            cascade at two different points

       Other antihypertensives during pregnancy
       Methyldopa is a pregnancy category A drug
       • Indicated in hypertension during pregnancy, gestational
         pregnancy, pre-eclampsia
           • not generally used in other settings
       • It is an alpha 2 agonist*
           • *not an antagonist, remember this receptor is the ‘negative feedback
             receptor’ that we put off to the side when talking about sympathomimetics –
             it does the opposite thing that we would expect a sympathomimetic to do
       • Methyldopa causes many psychological side effects and is
         normally not used outside pregnancy

                                 Diuretics

        • Inhibit sodium reabsorption from the kidney
        • Salt stays in the nephron, and is excreted along with the
          water that it pulls along with it.
        • Some diuretics actually result in compensation, thereby
          negating the blood pressure lowering effects.
        • Thiazide diuretics are a first line therapy for
          hypertension
        • Can be combined with other antihypertensive agents for
          greater efficacy.

## ppt/slides/slide55.xml
Diuretics 
Inhibit sodium reabsorption from the kidney
Salt stays in the nephron, and is excreted along with the water that it pulls along with it. 
Some diuretics actually result in compensation, thereby negating the blood pressure lowering effects. 
Thiazide diuretics are a first line therapy for hypertension
Can be combined with other antihypertensive agents for greater efficacy.
