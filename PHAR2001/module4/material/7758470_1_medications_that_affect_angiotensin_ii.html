Targeting the RAA mechanism in hypertension

The renin-angiotensin-aldosterone (RAA) mechanism is our 'one-stop-shop' for increasing our blood pressure. By simply releasing one enzyme, we can create a cascade of events that ultimately result in the blood pressure increasing in five different ways.

Wouldn't it be a great antihypertensive medication if we could design a drug that stopped that cascade? Well we did - twice ! This lesson describes two drug classes that ultimately decrease the effects of the RAA mechanism, and those medications are both 'first line' therapies for hypertension.

ACE inhibitors include Ramipril and other drugs that end in the …pril suffix

ACE inhibitors result in a chronic dry cough in 10 -35% of individuals who take the medications and if a client cannot tolerate the cough, they are given dextromethorphan which normally cures the cough.

The dry cough caused by ACE inhibitors cease immediately after treatment.

ACE inhibitors act as a type of diuretic, excreting more salt and water from the system, but are not considered diuretics because they have many more actions than diuresis.

ACE inhibitors are potassium wasting and they should be taken with potassium supplements.
