                        Haemostasis
           The process of haemostasis involves:
    1. Vessel spasm
    2. Formation of the platelet plug
    3. Blood coagulation or development of an insoluble
       fibrin clot
    4. Clot retraction
    5. Clot dissolution

                  Antiplatelet drugs: Aspirin
                     aka acetylsalicylic acid (ASA)
     • Aspirin used long-term to help prevent further heart
       attacks, ischaemic strokes, and arterial blood clots in
       people at high risk.
     • Aspirin works similarly to other NSAIDs but also affects
       platelets
     • The bark of the willow tree containing ASA has been used
       for its health effects for at least 2,400 years.
     • Clopidogrel is taking over as preferred antiplatelet drug
       • Can be combined with aspirin


19




                  Antiplatelet drugs: Aspirin
     • PGI2 from intact epithelium and TXA2 from platelets normally
       balance to form ideal haemostasis
       • This balance is disrupted when the intact epithelium is covered in
         atherosclerotic plaques, making the pro-platelet TXA2 dominate
     • The acetyl group in aspirin non competitively inhibits
       cyclooxygenase (COX), needed to form TXA2.
     • Thromboxane (TXA2) is therefore not able to be made for the
       entire 9 day life of the platelet.
       • Balance is restored
       • Intact endothelium contains PGI2; prostacyclin but the endothelial
         cells can synthesise more enzyme while platelets cannot


20




               Aspirin as an antiplatelet drug
     • Low dose aspirin (75-150 mg/day) is proven effective in
       reducing MI and strokes from atherosclerosis
     • Adverse effects
       • Stomach ache, stomach ulcers, allergies
     Q: I am healthy, and my arteries should be clean, why
     don’t I bleed readily when I take aspirin?
       • A: Aspirin doesn’t destroy platelets, it takes away one of their
         ‘tools’. Also, not all platelets are affected.

                             Clopidogrel
     • Clopidogrel is emerging as the preferred antiplatelet
       medication
     • Decreases risk of heart attack and stroke
     • Its effect starts about two hours after intake and lasts
       for five days.
     • Common side effects include headache, nausea, easy
       bruising, itching, and heartburn.
     • Rarely causes thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura.
     • Works by irreversibly inhibiting a receptor called P2Y12
       for ADP on platelets.

                          Anticoagulants
     • Anticoagulants, commonly known as blood
       thinners, but do not affect the viscosity of blood
     • Prevent or reduce coagulation of blood,
       prolonging the clotting time.
     • Used for both arterial and venous clots
     • One of the first anticoagulants, warfarin
        • Component from sweet clover
          • First developed as a poison: rat bait

                    Drugs that affect coagulation
                                        Warfarin
       • Taken orally, absorbed quickly
       • Takes several days to exert its effect (there is still active vitamin K
         hanging around)
       • Affects several clotting factors


       • Warfarin inhibits epoxide reductase, an enzyme that recycles vitamin
         K back to its active form
       • Without active vitamin K, many coagulation factors are missing,
         resulting in decreased coagulation and if excessive, bleeding

                 Drugs that affect coagulation
                        Warfarin (continued)
     • Dose is extremely variable (2-20mg/day) and requires
       frequent assessment of coagulation (INR tests need to
       be performed to make sure of ideal clotting)
     • Huge number of drugs, dietary factors affect its efficacy
     • Side effects
       • Allergy, bruising, excessive bleeding
       • WARFARIN IS TEROTAGENIC
          • (contraindicated in women of child bearing age)

                 Drugs that affect coagulation
                                 Rivaroxaban
     • An oral anticoagulant
     • Has fewer side effects than warfarin but greater chance of GIT
       bleeding
     • Works by blocking activity of clotting factor Xa
     • Pregnancy category C

                 Drugs that affect coagulation
                     Low molecular weight heparins

     • Injected subcutaneously like heparin, but without the major
       adverse effects


     • Safest anticoagulant to use in pregnancy (still a C drug but
       considered safer than rivaroxaban


     • All drugs of this class end in …parin
       • Enoxaparin (Clexane; know this drug)

                Fibrinolytic (thrombolytic) drugs
                                Streptokinase
     • Intravenously infused in a hospital setting.
     • Potent plasminogen activator
         • Increasing plasminogen increases plasmin activity. Plasmin is the
           enzyme that dissolves clots.
     • Easily results in haemorrhaging (narrow therapeutic range)
     • Allergies (of bacterial origin)
     • Cannot be re-administered for about 6 months due to allergies
       developing
         • Alteplase can be used if needed prior to 6 months
