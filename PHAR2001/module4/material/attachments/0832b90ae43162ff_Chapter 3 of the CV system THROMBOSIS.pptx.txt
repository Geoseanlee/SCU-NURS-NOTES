## ppt/slides/slide20.xml
Antiplatelet drugs: Aspirin
PGI2 from intact epithelium and TXA2 from platelets normally balance to form ideal haemostasis
This balance is disrupted when the intact epithelium is covered in atherosclerotic plaques, making the pro-platelet TXA2 dominate
The acetyl group in aspirin non competitively inhibits cyclooxygenase (COX), needed to form TXA2. 
Thromboxane (TXA2) is therefore not able to be made for the entire 9 day life of the platelet.
Balance is restored
Intact endothelium contains PGI2; prostacyclin but the endothelial cells can synthesise more enzyme while platelets cannot

## ppt/slides/slide21.xml
Aspirin as an antiplatelet drug
Low dose aspirin (75-150 mg/day) is proven effective in reducing MI and strokes from atherosclerosis
Adverse effects
Stomach ache, stomach ulcers, allergies
Q: I am healthy, and my arteries should be clean, why don’t I bleed readily when I take aspirin?
A: Aspirin doesn’t destroy platelets, it takes away one of their ‘tools’. Also, not all platelets are affected. 

## ppt/slides/slide22.xml
Clopidogrel
Clopidogrel is emerging as the preferred antiplatelet medication 
Decreases risk of heart attack and stroke
Its effect starts about two hours after intake and lasts for five days.
Common side effects include headache, nausea, easy bruising, itching, and heartburn.
Rarely causes thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura.
Works by irreversibly inhibiting a receptor called P2Y12 for ADP on platelets.

## ppt/slides/slide24.xml
Anticoagulants
Anticoagulants, commonly known as blood thinners, but do not affect the viscosity of blood
Prevent or reduce coagulation of blood, prolonging the clotting time.
Used for both arterial and venous clots
One of the first anticoagulants, warfarin
Component from sweet clover
First developed as a poison: rat bait

## ppt/slides/slide27.xml
Drugs that affect coagulation
Warfarin
Taken orally, absorbed quickly
Takes several days to exert its effect (there is still active vitamin K hanging around)
Affects several clotting factors
Warfarin inhibits epoxide reductase, an enzyme that recycles vitamin K back to its active form
Without active vitamin K, many coagulation factors are missing, resulting in decreased coagulation and if excessive, bleeding 

## ppt/slides/slide28.xml
Drugs that affect coagulation
Warfarin (continued)
Dose is extremely variable (2-20mg/day) and requires frequent assessment of coagulation (INR tests need to be performed to make sure of ideal clotting)
Huge number of drugs, dietary factors affect its efficacy
Side effects
Allergy, bruising, excessive bleeding
WARFARIN IS TEROTAGENIC  
(contraindicated in women of child bearing age)

## ppt/slides/slide32.xml
Fibrinolytic (thrombolytic) drugs
Streptokinase 
Intravenously infused in a hospital setting. 
Potent plasminogen activator 
Increasing plasminogen increases plasmin activity. Plasmin is the enzyme that dissolves clots. 
Easily results in haemorrhaging (narrow therapeutic range)
Allergies (of bacterial origin)
Cannot be re-administered for about 6 months due to allergies developing 
Alteplase can be used if needed prior to 6 months
