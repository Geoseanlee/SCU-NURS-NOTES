                   Hypertension
           A consistently elevated blood pressure
           equal to or greater than 140/90mmHg

     Primary/essential hypertension (90-95% of cases)
     • Many causes:
        • Lifestyle factors
        • Genetics
     Secondary hypertension
     • One cause
        • Underlying disease process
        • Drugs
        • Hormonal factors

                 Understanding the Renin Cascade
          • Think of renin as the panic button to simultaneously
            increase blood pressure by every means available

          • When our blood pressure is too low, or there is sympathetic
            nervous system stimulation to the kidneys, renin is
            released.

          • The ultimate effect is an increase of the blood pressure by
            at least 5 mechanisms

          • Our medications of the renin cascade attempt to stop this
            cascade at two different points

                    Medications of the Renin Cascade
                      Renin-angiotensin aldosterone system
           • ACE inhibitors inhibit the conversion of angiotensin I to
             angiotensin II
           • ARBs antagonise the Angiotensin II receptors

           • Both:
              • Decrease aldosterone the salt saver
              • Decrease ADH, the water saver
              • Decreases vasoconstriction
              • Increase fluid released from kidneys
              • Decrease the renin-controlled SNS ‘positive feedback’ loop

          • Ramipril and other …PRIL drugs
          • Indicated in congestive heart disease and hypertension
          • Adverse effects:

              • Hyperkalaemia
          • Contraindicated in pregnancy (Category D)

                              Beta blockers
            Beta blockers are sympathetic antagonists,
              sympatholytics, beta receptor blockers
          • Example: Atenolol and other …OLOL drugs
          • Block Beta 1 receptor sites on the heart
          • Decreased sympathetic stimulation to the heart
          • Decreased heart rate (negative chronotropic effect)
          • Decreased contractility (negative inotropic effect)
       • There are at least 4 drug classes that are better!
       • Taper these drugs off slowly or a rebound hypertension
         may develop

       Other antihypertensives during pregnancy
       Methyldopa is a pregnancy category A drug
       • Indicated in hypertension during pregnancy, gestational
         pregnancy, pre-eclampsia
           • not generally used in other settings
       • It is an alpha 2 agonist*
           • *not an antagonist, remember this receptor is the ‘negative feedback
             receptor’ that we put off to the side when talking about sympathomimetics –
             it does the opposite thing that we would expect a sympathomimetic to do
       • Methyldopa causes many psychological side effects and is
         normally not used outside pregnancy

                                 Diuretics

        • Inhibit sodium reabsorption from the kidney
        • Salt stays in the nephron, and is excreted along with the
          water that it pulls along with it.
        • Some diuretics actually result in compensation, thereby
          negating the blood pressure lowering effects.
        • Thiazide diuretics are a first line therapy for
          hypertension
        • Can be combined with other antihypertensive agents for
          greater efficacy.

        Thiazide diuretics (hydrochlorothiazide)

 • Inhibit sodium reabsorption from the distal tubule
 • Wastes potassium as well
 • Used for hypertension, congestive heart failure, nephrolithiasis and diabetes
   insipidus
 • Thiazide diuretics compete with the secretion of uric acid so they are
   contraindicated in people with gout


 • Several drugs use hydrochlorothiazide in combination to prevent – a little bit
   increases the action of other
     Hydrochlorothiazide (Dithiazide)
