Alternative diuretics

The material in this lesson will not be assessed in PHAR2001.  

The final lesson in the Cardiovascular and Renal system pharmacology has to do with the miscellaneous diuretics, the most important of which are the osmotic diuretics. These are not generally used to decrease fluid volume but rather they are used in special circumstances like intracranial hypertension and glaucoma. They are completely different to other types of diuretics and as such it is interesting to compare the actions and administration of these to other diuretics.

The type of diuretic that would be used in cases of intracranial hypertension from head injury or glaucoma would be one of the osmotic diuretics such as mannitol.

Most osmotic diuretics need to be injected, and once in the system, they directly pull fluid from interstitial spaces. This differs from other diuretics that mainly excrete fluid in the urine, and then the decreased fluids in the blood allow the fluid in congested areas to slowly normalise.

The osmotic diuretics including mannitol are a strong diuretic, pulling a lot of fluid out of the system and allowing excretion

Mannitol is prescribed at a dose of two 500mg tablets per day, one at night and one in the morning.
