## Lipids and atherosclerosis

Connect lipid transport, vascular injury and plaque formation rather than labelling every lipid as harmful. Explain the intended effects of statins and the additional lipid-modifying approaches in the chapter, including reducing cholesterol absorption.

## Blood-pressure regulation

Use cardiac output, vascular resistance, fluid balance and the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system to understand treatment. The chapter’s A/B/C/D organising framework is a learning aid, not a complete prescribing algorithm.

## Antihypertensive mechanisms

Compare alpha blockade, beta blockade, ACE inhibition/angiotensin-receptor blockade, calcium-channel blockade and diuresis. Link each to a physiological control point and likely safety monitoring. Comorbidity and current guidelines affect selection.

## Three different antithrombotic jobs

Antiplatelet drugs affect platelet activation; anticoagulants affect coagulation; fibrinolytic drugs promote clot breakdown. They are not interchangeable descriptions of a single blood-thinning action. Relate all three to bleeding risk and the clinical indication.

## Renal pharmacology

Map nephron transport and fluid/electrolyte handling before comparing thiazides, loop diuretics, potassium-sparing agents and other diuretics. Preserve the source’s non-compulsory labels for the additional lessons. Monitor the reasoning linking site of action to changes in volume and electrolytes.

## Revision checks

Explain the difference between preventing platelet aggregation and breaking down a clot; trace the effects of changing angiotensin II; compare thiazide and loop sites of action; link diuresis with blood-pressure and heart-failure goals. No individual treatment recommendation is implied.

## Test the explanation, not only the name

Explain how reducing angiotensin II activity differs from blocking a calcium channel. Explain why an antiplatelet does not simply dissolve an established clot. Draw the nephron and map the classes in the course; mark loop, potassium-sparing and other extensions with the source’s optional labels. A successful explanation connects the site of action, expected physiological change and monitoring question.
