Module overview

Module 1: The basics of Pharmacology
Hi All,
Module 1 explores most of the important basics of Pharmacology to give you a solid foundation for your understanding of medications in the future. Normally, the foundational information in pharmacology is the most difficult (and sometimes boring) material.
But in this module, we use the medications of the gastrointestinal system/tract (GIT) to explore these difficult topics, making it actually fun. We start out with the easiest of medications - those that cannot get absorbed and work by chemical action or by direct action. (Normally, our medications work on cellular receptors or affect enzymes, but the first topic/chapter introduces you very slowly.) Next, Topic/Chapter 2, we explore cellular receptors, looking at what they are, what they do and how our drugs can affect them. This is where we get into the topic of pharmacodynamics. The third topic/chapter starts introducing you to pharmacokinetics, the movement of drugs through the system, involving absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion. We look at enzymes and how we can affect them with our medications in Topic/chapter 4. Finally, in Topic/chapter 5, we look at neurotransmitters and the most difficult concepts in the GIT, which have to do with our antiemetics.
If you want to love pharmacology the way I do, please pay close attention to this first module. I teamed up with a partner in this module to make sure that you can actually picture the processes that will form the basis of your understanding of pharmacology. Unfortunately, while all of the videos in this unit are of relatively high quality, this module will have a significantly higher level of annimation than subsequent modules, but by then you will have developed the ability of picturing how our medications work !
Enjoy! Regards,
Ken
Module purpose
By the end of this module you will be able to:
- Explain the main principles of pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics
- Describe the mechanism of action, pharmacological effects, therapuetic uses, contraindications, safety issues and side effects of the most important medications of the GIT

Stimulant laxatives
If you have ever had a GIT 'bug' and ended up going to the toilet about 20 times, you may have wondered why that was happening. ===== FILE: 17 - Affinity potency selectivity.epub =====
Affinity potency selectivity
-
-
Understanding affinity, potency and selectivity
Why is it that medication A, which works in the same way as Medication B can be so much stronger (more potent)? While there can be many reasons, one of the most common has to do with the strength of the molecular bond of the medication to its given receptor.
Most often, a medication that affects cellular receptors binds to its receptor and then lets go shortly after. Then it binds to another receptor of the same type, and lets go... But what if the drug bound to the receptor and it really takes a lot more time to get it off that receptor (it had a much greater affinity to the receptor)? As you can imagine, many more messages will be sent to the cell. So the drug's affinity can affect its potency.
This is another really important lesson as we learn three very important concepts, affinity, potency and selectivity.

===== FILE: 24 - Metabolism of drugs.epub =====
Metabolism of drugs
-
-
The importance of the liver
The liver is the site of most metabolism of drugs. As soon as most drugs are absorbed and begin the distribution process, our body wants to excrete the drug as quickly as possible. And since the kidneys are the main site of excretion of most drugs, the main goal of metabolism of drugs is to make the drug more water soluble and easy to excrete. We want that drug out of there ASAP
Interestingly, as drugs go through the liver, many different scenarios are possible, including that an inactive pro-drug can become an active drug or an active drug can become an inactive drug. This lesson explains the basics of the third principle of Pharmacokinetics, the metabolism of drugs, in a way that you can apply the information to any medication.

===== FILE: 28 - Histamine 2 blockers.epub =====
Histamine 2 blockers
-
-
Safety issue
The most popular Histamine 2 receptor blockers (due to better efficacy and potency) was Ranitidine (Zantac) until 2020 when it was discovered that some components of the drug broke down into carcinogenic components. This happened to a greater extent over time and if the drug was stored improperly, like in a hot car. The company that manufactured Zantac has changed the drug from Ranitidine to Famotidine and changed the brand name of their version of Famotidine to Zantac 360. Clients who mention having Zantac should be questioned to make sure they are not taking leftover Ranitidine.

===== FILE: 42 - Neurotransmitters.epub =====
Neurotransmitters
-
-
What are neurotransmitters?
Neurons communicate with the adjacent neuron using a neurotransmitter that excites or inhibits the next neuron. But we need to get that neurotransmitter back out of the area very quickly by a number of methods, so that there is not constant excitation or inhibition at the next neuron. Our medications that affect neurotransmission can target the receptors on the neurons, they can mimic the neurotransmitter, or they can target the ways in which we get the neurotransmitters out of the synaptic cleft. The antiemetics all target the receptors on the neurons. In other words, they are receptor antagonists for the neurotransmitters involved in emesis.
