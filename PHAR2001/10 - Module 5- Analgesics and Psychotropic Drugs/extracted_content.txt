Overview of Module 5: Analgesics and psychotropic drugs
Welcome to Module 5 where we will be discussing the analgesics and the psychotropic drugs. As you probably already know, the analgesics are pain killers and the psychotropic drugs are drugs that affect behaviour, mood, thoughts or perception. While these two groups of medications initially seem like they would both be working at the level of the brain, most of the analgesics actually work somewhere along the pathways in which the pain is carried. Obviously, the stimuli that are carried along that pathway are ultimately perceived by the brain as pain.
These two topics are extremely important and include a disproportional amount of medications in the top 200 most prescribed. For instance, 12 of the top 100 most prescribed medications are antidepressants. When you are ready, click on the analgesics portion of this module and start furthering your understanding of pain.

- Define chronic pain and neuropathic pain and discuss the most common medications used to treat that type of pain
- Name and describe the most common pharmacological agents used in the treatment of Depression, Anxiety, Seizures, and Schizophrenia, including their indications, contraindications, mechanisms of action and side effects.

Understanding Inflammation and pain introduction
-
-
The physiology and pathology of pain
Pain is a protective mechanism. It tells us when we need to tend to injuries, and helps further complications develop on those injuries. But when pain becomes chronic, there are permanent changes that magnify and perpetuate the pain. This has led to chronic pain now being considered a disorder in its own right. Given the importance of pain and the percentage of individuals living in chronic pain (20% of Australians), it is important that we learn as much about pain as we can.

===== FILE: 06 - Paracetamol (Acetaminophen).epub =====
Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)
-
-
Paracetamol: mechanisms, effects, and limitations
Contrary to the NSAIDs, Paracetamol, which is called Acetaminophen (Tylenol) in some countries does not have anti-inflammatory properties. (In this instance, we actually do need to know the other names of Paracetamol, because overseas clients will often be confused as to why their specific analgesic is not available in Australia.) Paracetamol does have anti-pyretic effects but only has only a fraction of the amount of analgesic effects as compared to NSAIDs. This brings up an important point, because most people think that, because it is an analgesic, if they are not getting the pain relief that they expect from the first dose, they should just take more. But a weak analgesic does not get more effective with a greater dose – there is just more toxic effects on the liver! Paracetamol toxicity is the number one cause of acute liver damage in Australia, so we should know as much about paracetamol as possible.

Pain relief in children and elderly

Pain management for the elderly and children
In this lesson, we will be learning about the management of pain in children and the elderly. Both of those groups of people have a decreased ability to metabolise and excrete medications. But in addition, there are other specific issue for both groups. For instance, one would normally think that if the dose for a 100 kg adult is 100 mg, the 10 kg child should take 10 mg. But that dose could cause serious harm or death of the child, because dose for children is not calculated that way. For the elderly, they are often on several medications at the same time so that, combined with their inability to metabolise the medications quickly will lead to special issues. ===== FILE: 15 - Anxiety.epub =====
Anxiety
-
-
Benzodiazepines
Right now, we are going to explore the Anxiolytics and hypnotics. Anxiolytics are medications that reduce anxiety and hypnotics are medications that help the client sleep. Often the anxiolytics are useful hypnotics. The benzodiazepines are probably the most important groups of medications. You have probably heard of Zanax (Alprazolam) and/or Valium (Diazepam). Those are two benzodiazepines that are in the top 200 most prescribed medications but Clonazepam and Lorazepam are also benzodiazepines that are highly prescribed. The benzo’s as you may nickname them in the future are variable with respect to the half-life and often the exact types of disorders that they have been proven to treat. For instance, some like alprazolam have antidepressant actions, and a half-life of 11 hours. While that may seem long, others such as Diazepam have a much longer half-life of 50 hours. Remember, the longer the half-life the greater risk of developing toxicity, so it is important to look out for that with the longer-acting Benzos.

The need for alternative medications
Because of the fact that the benzodiazepines are addictive, are often abused, and are associated with an increased risk of developing Alzheimer’s disease, we are always eager to see alternative treatments for anxiety. Introduction to Depression
We are going to shift our attention to depression right now. Depression is a very common disorder that is very much linked to stress, as stress seems to dysregulate the area of the brain associated with mood. The present hypothesis is that the person who is depressed normally has a (relative) decrease in the levels level of serotonin and/or noradrenaline in the areas of the brain associated with mood. SNRIs and atypical antidepressants
-
-
Introduction to SNRIs
While SSRIs are the most commonly prescribed group of antidepressants, there is a newer group of medications that works in a similar way to the SSRIs but affects both serotonin and noradrenaline. The medication is called an SNRI (Serotonin Noradrenaline Reuptake Inhibitor). The side effect profile is similar, but in addition to the antidepressant effects, the SNRIs may have an effect reducing chronic and neuropathic pain. There are also what we call ‘atypical antidepressants’ that tend to work at multiple sites.

===== FILE: 22 - Seizures (Not compulsory for this unit.epub =====
Seizures (Not compulsory for this unit)
-
-
Antiseizure medications
The material in this lesson is not compulsory in PHAR2001.
This last lesson deals with anti-seizure medications. A seizure is defined as a sudden and uncontrollable surge of electrical activity in the brain. There are several different types of seizures, but the most important thing is to distinguish between a partial (or focal) seizure which does not cross into both hemispheres of the brain and a generalised seizure, which does cross into the other side of the brain, with the client losing consciousness. When a person gets recurrent seizures of either type, the person is said to have epilepsy. Antiseizure medications need to break into the blood brain barrier and decrease positive ions (Sodium or calcium) in the brain or increase negative ions (chloride) in the brain. As you would expect, this would normally result in sleepiness and possibly a bit of mental confusion, but nonetheless, seizures are very important to treat.
