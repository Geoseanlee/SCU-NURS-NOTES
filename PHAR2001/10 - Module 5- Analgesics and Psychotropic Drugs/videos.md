## PHAR2001_5_2_2_NSAIDs_and_COX2_Inhibitors
- Link: https://scu.au.panopto.com/Panopto/Pages/Viewer.aspx?id=a498ea10-f07f-4ac3-a9d0-b47b00fb0274
- Source: Panopto
- Duration: 7:24

**AI summary**

Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) and aspirin are widely used over-the-counter medications that share a similar mechanism of action by inhibiting the cyclooxygenase (Cox) enzymes, which are necessary for the production of inflammatory prostaglandins. However, this inhibition can also lead to adverse effects, particularly on the gastrointestinal and renal systems. NSAIDs and aspirin can increase the risk of peptic ulcers by reducing the protective prostaglandins in the stomach, and they can also cause renal toxicity by interfering with the prostaglandins that regulate blood flow in the kidneys. Additionally, chronic use of NSAIDs has been associated with an increased risk of hypertension and myocardial infarction, although aspirin does not share this increased cardiovascular risk. The development of selective Cox-2 inhibitors, such as celecoxib and parecoxib, was intended to mitigate these side effects by targeting only the Cox-2 enzyme and leaving the Cox-1 enzyme unaffected. However, these drugs have also been found to carry a high risk of serious cardiovascular complications, and they are no more effective than traditional NSAIDs. As a result, the use of NSAIDs and aspirin, even over-the-counter, should be carefully considered and limited to the smallest effective dose for the shortest duration possible to minimize the potential for adverse effects.

## PHAR2001_5_2_3_Paracetamol_Acetaminophen
- Link: https://scu.au.panopto.com/Panopto/Pages/Viewer.aspx?id=dde3b15b-5ccc-4865-9ba6-b47b0062b3d0
- Source: Panopto
- Duration: 4:49

**AI summary**

Paracetamol, also known as acetaminophen or Tylenol, is a widely used pain reliever and fever reducer. While it is thought to provide about half the pain relief of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, it does not have any anti-inflammatory properties. The exact mechanism of how paracetamol works is still not fully understood, but it is believed to act on the central nervous system. Current guidelines recommend paracetamol as the first-line treatment for mild to moderate pain, although some researchers have questioned its efficacy. One of the most significant concerns about paracetamol is that it is the leading cause of acute liver toxicity in several countries, including Australia, the UK, the USA, and Canada. This is due to several factors, such as the variable half-life of the drug, the tendency for people to take more when the pain persists, and the combination with opioids. Additionally, paracetamol is a common choice for suicide attempts, despite the fact that it can lead to a particularly painful and prolonged death due to acute liver failure. Healthcare professionals must be aware of these risks and educate their clients accordingly, as there is a narrow window of opportunity to intervene and prevent severe liver damage in cases of paracetamol overdose.
