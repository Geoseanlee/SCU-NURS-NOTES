## 1. Evolution of Nursing in Australia

Nursing in Australia developed from informal caregiving, religious and charitable work, and
hospital-based learning into a university-prepared, regulated profession.

### Key milestones
- Early colonial practice: basic care, often informal and under-supported
- Nightingale-trained nurses arriving in 1868 introduced sanitation and structured training
- Hospital-based apprenticeship model for nursing education
- Registration and regulation to protect the public
- Transition to university education and evidence-based practice

### Nursing today
Modern nursing is grounded in:
- evidence and research
- ethical decision-making
- accountability and professional standards
- person-centred care
- critical thinking and clinical judgement

## 2. Nursing Theories

Nursing theories help explain what nursing is and how nurses should think, assess and care.
They guide practice and support clinical reasoning.

### Common concepts in nursing theory
- person
- health
- environment
- nursing role

### Importance of theory
- guides practice and assessment
- supports professional identity
- provides a language for inquiry and reflection
- aligns care with the person’s context and needs

---

## 3. Person-Centred Care and the 6Cs

Person-centred care places the individual at the centre of decision-making and care.

### Core principles
- respect for personhood
- therapeutic relationship
- holistic care
- shared decision-making
- dignity and autonomy

### 6Cs of care
- Care
- Compassion
- Competence
- Communication
- Courage
- Commitment

These values support safe, compassionate and person-centred nursing.

---

## 4. Self-Care, Emotional Intelligence and Reflective Practice

Nurses must support their own wellbeing to maintain capability for practice.

### Self-care includes
- sleep and recovery
- emotional regulation
- boundaries and workload awareness
- supportive relationships and mentorship

### Emotional intelligence
Includes self-awareness, self-regulation, empathy and social skills. These support professional
behaviour, therapeutic communication and stress management.

### Reflective practice
Reflection helps nurses learn from experience, identify patterns, improve practice and prevent
burnout. Models may support structured reflection.

---

## 5. Ethics, Law and Professional Practice

Nursing requires ethical reasoning and legal awareness.

### Ethics includes
- beneficence
- non-maleficence
- autonomy
- justice
- confidentiality

### Consent and capacity
A person must give informed consent to treatment when able. Capacity is decision-specific and
must be assessed appropriately. If a person lacks capacity, care should follow legal and ethical
guidance and involve appropriate decision-makers.

### Privacy and confidentiality
Nurses must protect information and use it only for legitimate clinical purposes.

---

## 6. Professionalism and Regulation

Professionalism in nursing includes accountability, competence, standards, communication and
ethical conduct.

### Regulatory framework in Australia
- AHPRA
- Nursing and Midwifery Board of Australia (NMBA)
- Registered Nurse Standards for Practice
- Codes, policies, laws and organisational procedures

### Professional responsibilities
- maintain competence
- scope of practice
- follow legal and ethical standards
- document accurately
- escalate concerns responsibly

---

## 7. Interprofessional Education and Teamwork

Healthcare is collaborative. Effective practice requires teamwork across professions.

### Benefits of interprofessional work
- better coordination of care
- reduced errors and duplication
- improved patient experience and outcomes
- shared responsibility for patient safety

### Nursing role
- communicate clearly
- understand roles and responsibilities of others
- participate in handover and teamwork

---

## 8. Infection Prevention and Control

Infection prevention is central to patient safety.

### Core concepts
- chain of infection
- standard precautions
- hand hygiene
- PPE
- sharps safety
- environmental cleanliness
- transmission-based precautions where required

### Standard precautions apply to all patients
- hand hygiene
- risk assessment
- gloves, masks, gowns or eye protection as required
- safe sharps practice
- appropriate cleaning and waste disposal

---

## 9. Manual Handling, Mobility and Positioning

Nursing care often involves moving and positioning patients. Safe manual handling prevents injury
and supports patient dignity and comfort.

### Key principles
- assess the task, person and environment
- use equipment and assistive devices when needed
- maintain safe body mechanics
- support patient comfort and independence
- screen for falls and pressure injury risk

### Common issues
- back strain among staff
- falls and pressure injuries in patients
- reduced mobility and immobility complications

---

## 10. Communication and Clinical Reasoning

Communication is essential to safe care. A common structure is ISBAR.

### ISBAR
- I — Introduction
- S — Situation
- B — Background
- A — Assessment
- R — Recommendation

### Clinical reasoning cycle
Clinical reasoning guides nurses to:
- collect cues
- process information
- identify problems
- establish goals
- take action
- evaluate outcomes

This supports safe, thoughtful nursing decisions.
