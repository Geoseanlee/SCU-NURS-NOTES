# NURS5010 Module 3 Notes
## Nutrition, Hydration and Fluid Balance

> This module introduces the foundations of nutritional care, hydration assessment, homeostasis,
> fluid balance, and documentation. Accurate charting and observation are essential to patient
> safety and clinical decision-making.

---

## 0. Module Overview

**Key topics:**
- nutrition basics and nutrients
- factors affecting nutrition
- assessing nutritional status
- oral and alternative feeding
- homeostasis
- body water balance
- fluid deficit and fluid overload
- fluid balance charting and documentation

**Nursing focus:**
- identify nutritional risk
- support intake and feeding needs
- recognise dehydration and overload
- maintain accurate intake/output records
- document clearly and in a timely manner

---

## 1. Nutrition Fundamentals

Nutrition provides the body with energy and the building blocks for function, growth, repair and
healing.

### Major nutrient groups
- carbohydrates
- proteins
- fats
- vitamins
- minerals
- water

### Why it matters
Nutrition affects recovery, immunity, muscle function, wound healing and overall health.
Illness, surgery, chronic disease, poverty, access barriers and culture can all affect intake and
nutritional risk.

---

## 2. Factors Affecting Nutrition

Many factors influence nutritional status, including:
- illness or surgery
- appetite and nausea
- dysphagia or chewing difficulty
- mental health and stress
- social and economic factors
- culture and food preference
- access to food and resources

### Nursing responsibility
Assess dietary intake, ability to eat, barriers to intake, and the person’s cultural and personal
preferences.

---

## 3. Nutritional Assessment

A nutritional assessment helps identify malnutrition or risk of malnutrition.

### Assessment may include
- diet history
- appetite and oral intake
- weight and recent changes
- signs of weakness, dehydration or poor wound healing
- laboratory data when relevant
- swallowing and oral health concerns

### Why it matters
Early detection supports early intervention and reduces complications.

---

## 4. Alternative Feeding Methods

Some patients cannot safely eat or absorb enough nutrients by mouth.

### Common routes
- Nasogastric tube (NGT)
- Percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy (PEG)

### Nursing considerations
- check tube position and patency when indicated
- monitor tolerance, aspiration risk and gastric output
- maintain hygiene and documentation
- watch for complications

---

## 5. Homeostasis and Fluid Balance

Homeostasis is the body’s attempt to keep a stable internal environment.

### Fluid balance is essential for
- circulation
- tissue perfusion
- blood pressure regulation
- cellular function
- temperature control

### Body fluid compartments
Cells, interstitial space and intravascular space all contain water and dissolved substances.
Fluids shift between compartments depending on needs, illness, and treatment.

---

## 6. Fluid Imbalance

### Fluid overload (hypervolaemia)
Can occur with heart failure, kidney disease, liver disease, excess IV fluid or sodium retention.

**Signs:**
- oedema
- shortness of breath
- raised blood pressure
- weight gain
- crackles on auscultation

### Fluid deficit (hypovolaemia)
Can occur with vomiting, diarrhoea, bleeding, burns, fever, poor intake or diuretic use.

**Signs:**
- thirst
- dry mucous membranes
- tachycardia
- dizziness
- reduced urine output
- low blood pressure in severe cases

---

## 7. Fluid Balance Charting

A fluid balance chart records input and output over a set period.

### Intake includes
- oral fluids
- IV fluids
- tube feeds
- fluid in food (where relevant)

### Output includes
- urine
- vomit
- drains
- stool losses and wound exudate if applicable

### Nursing priority
Accurate charting is essential for ongoing care, response to treatment and escalation of concern.

---

## 8. Documentation and Patient Safety

Fluid charts are clinical records. They support communication, continuity of care and legal
accountability.

### Documentation principles
- accurate
- timely
- clear and legible
- complete and sequential
- consistent with patient condition

Poor charting may lead to misinterpretation, delayed treatment and harm.

---

## 9. Key Takeaways

- Nutrition and hydration are basic but critical aspects of care.
- Homeostasis depends on balance between intake, output and regulation.
- Early recognition of imbalance improves patient outcomes.
- Nurses must document accurately and act on concerning trends.
