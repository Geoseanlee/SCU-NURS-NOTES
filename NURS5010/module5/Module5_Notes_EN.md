## 1. Personal Hygiene

Personal hygiene care includes washing, grooming, oral care, hair care, skincare and toileting
assistance. It supports comfort, dignity and infection prevention.

### Importance of hygiene care
- supports skin integrity and comfort
- reduces infection risk
- improves self-esteem and dignity
- helps patients feel clean, safe and respected

### Nursing considerations
- respect privacy and consent
- assess mobility, pain, cognitive status and preference
- adapt care to the person’s abilities and culture
- explain tasks clearly and provide reassurance

---

## 2. Nursing Interventions to Promote Hygiene

Nurses may assist with:
- bed bathing or assisted washing
- oral and denture care
- hair care and grooming
- skincare and pressure area care
- managing incontinence-related hygiene needs

### Safe practice points
- maintain infection prevention standards
- use equipment appropriately
- observe skin integrity and discomfort
- document care and changes in condition

---

## 3. Sleep and Rest

Sleep is a basic physiological need. It supports healing, emotional regulation, memory and daytime
functioning.

### Factors affecting sleep and rest
- pain
- anxiety or stress
- noise and light
- unfamiliar environment
- medications
- illness or discomfort
- poor positioning or incontinence
- environmental disturbances in hospital

### Nursing interventions
- reduce avoidable noise and disruption
- support comfort and timely hygiene care
- optimise pain management and rest periods
- maintain a calming environment where possible

---

## 4. Hospital Sleep Challenges

Hospital environments can disturb sleep. Common barriers include:
- overnight lighting and noise
- regular observations and interruptions
- unfamiliar equipment and alarms
- pain or discomfort
- environmental discomfort and anxiety

### Nursing response
- cluster care when possible
- protect rest periods
- minimise unnecessary disruption
- use patient-centred communication and reassurance

---

## 5. Sensory Impairment

Sensory impairment may involve hearing, sight, touch, smell or balance. It can affect communication,
orientation, mobility and emotional wellbeing.

### Common issues
- reduced hearing or vision
- sensory overload
- confusion or disorientation
- fear and withdrawal
- impaired communication and safety risk

### Nursing strategies
- identify the patient’s communication needs
- use clear, calm communication
- adapt lighting, positioning and environment
- check for hearing aids or glasses
- involve family and carers as appropriate

---

## 6. Final Reflection and Person-Centred Care

Comfort, sleep, hygiene and sensory wellbeing are not “extras.” They are fundamental care needs
that affect recovery, dignity and quality of life.

### Good nursing care includes
- listening to patient preference and routine
- reducing distress and unnecessary disruption
- adapting care to the individual
- recognising the impact of environment on recovery

---

## 7. Key Takeaways

- Hygiene, rest and sensory wellbeing are core nursing care concerns.
- Sleep disruption in hospital can affect recovery and mental wellbeing.
- Sensory impairment requires individualised, respectful communication.
- Comfort-focused care improves safety, dignity and healing.
